Exam Rules and Academic Integrity

The exam is 1.5 hours and the only allowable resources you can use during the exam are:

- Notes you took yourself (either on paper or electronically - notes on a tablet computer are fine)

- Notes from the course (powerpoint or PDF files I have posted on Canvas, including the steam tables)

- The textbook (Turns and Pauley)

- A calculator (a physical calculator or one on your computer)

- Dr. O'Connor - if you have questions during the exam, use the Chat function in Zoom to chat ONLY ME (not everyone, that's distracting) and I will answer your question via chat. 

Resources you are NOT allowed to use during the exam are:

- Course videos - you won't have time to re-watch things and so I'm putting this out of bounds. You'll need every minute I give you on the exam.

- Searching the internet for any answers

- Any course-help websites like Chegg, CourseHero, or OCW

- Any generative AI, including ChatGTP or other programs like it

- You may not communicate with anyone other than Dr. O'Connor during the exam, including people inside OR outside the course, about the exam or course material

- Obviously, you must do your own exam. Someone cannot do the exam for you or help you with the problems.

I take academic integrity extremely seriously and I'm allowing a very generous policy on notes and help from me (through Zoom) during the exam to give you no reason to have to cheat during the exam. I will follow university policy of reporting suspected cases of academic integrity violations directly to the College of Engineering. For real, folks, just don't do it. I'm going to help you prepare for the exam so much, I want to be a real resource, so do the right thing and prepare well, and it will all be great. I'm a big fan of partial credit in my grading, so there's really no reason to cheat.

Exam Structure

The exam has three parts: concepts, short problems, and one long problem. The practice exams below are representative of that structure. The goal of the concept questions are to ensure that you understand concepts - you'll have to explain a graph, answer a question about relative magnitudes, explain processes, draw sketches, etc. The goal of the second section is to hit a few calculations that aren't captured in the final problem. The final problem tests your ability to work through a more complex thermodynamic scenario. It will have multiple steps and will be the longest part of the exam. Make sure you save time for this part, as it's also worth the most points.

The exam will be administered in two parts on Canvas:

- Part 1: A Canvas "quiz" with all the questions and spaces for the answers. There will also be a downloadable PDF with all the questions and spaces for the answers (just like the examples below) if you want to also have something to write on. All questions will be visible for the entire duration of the exam. You will answer the questions and if asked, type your answers into the answer boxes in Canvas. The questions will have very clear instructions as to what goes in the box and what goes on your written work - see the practice exam on Canvas for an example. This portion of the exam will last 1.5 hours.

- Part 2: A second Canvas "quiz" with only one question where you will upload a copy of your written work. Before the exam, download an app like CamScanner and upload your exam as a PDF file. Practice this process before the exam - it is much easier for me to grade and comment on a PDF file. This portion of the exam will be open for 15 minutes only. I expect that you do NOT keep working on the exam during this time. I will only grade problems that have final answers in Part 1 - anything that is in Part 2 but does not have an entry in the box in Part 2 will not be counted. You must upload written work - I will give partial credit based on this upload and will only give 50% credit for numerical answers in Part 1 that don't have work associated with them in Part 2. It will be very clear from the questions which ones require written work.
