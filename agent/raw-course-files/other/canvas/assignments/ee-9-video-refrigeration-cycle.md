# EE 9 Video - Refrigeration Cycle

Canvas assignment. Points: 100.0. Due (Summer 2023): None. Submission: online_url.

Schedule a time with your partner (listed below) for a Zoom meeting where you'll record your Explained Example activity. You should only meet once - do not meet to "rehearse" this activity. The goal is to record your spontaneous interactions and thought processes.

Research by Brent and Felder (https://psu.instructure.com/courses/2492426/files/192510254?wrap=1) has shown that this activity can help students retain information much better than watching someone else do the example. The goals of this activity are to provide you an opportunity to:

- See another example of the problem-solving techniques and concepts in practice

- Strengthen your conceptual understanding underlying problem-solving methods

- Resolve questions and clarify misunderstandings with a partner

- Develop problem solving skills by both working through the solution provided by the professor and working with a partner to solve the final part of the problem

For this assignment, follow these directions:

- Start a Zoom meeting and start recording. Make sure one of you has shared the screen with the problem on it.

- Decide which of you will be Speaker A or Speaker B.

- In the problem below, each section of the problem is marked with a speaker (A or B) who should “present” the problem to the other partner. Present the problem like you were teaching it, explaining what’s happening and why. You should try to present your example with the same depth of explanations that Dr. O'Connor does in the course videos when she does an example. Watch the example video of Dr. O'Connor and Erica Winegardner doing an Explained Example - you can find it on the Explained Example page (https://psu.instructure.com/courses/2492426/pages/explained-example-videos).

- The other partner should then ask questions about that portion of the problem, ensuring that both partners understand the step before moving onto the next part and switching roles. If the presenting student gets stuck, the other student can help out. If both of you are stuck, leave that part of the problem. 

- It's entirely fine if you can't explain part of the problem - you'll reflect on it in the second part of the assignment.

- Submit the sharable Zoom link to the assignment so that I can watch the video. The grade is based on completeness, not how correct your answers are. Do not rehearse the discussion - I want to see how you work through the problem without having seen it before.
