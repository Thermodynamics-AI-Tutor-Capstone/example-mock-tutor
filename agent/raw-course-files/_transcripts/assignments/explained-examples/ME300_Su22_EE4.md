**EXPLAINED EXAMPLE 4 – STEADY FLOW ENERGY EQUATION**

[handwritten] ①

An inventor is trying to develop a better industrial hair dryer for salons where a central heat exchanger heats the air and disperses it to a number of different drying stations. Her prototype for a small salon has four dryers with identical nozzles, as is shown in the figure below. Your job in the patent office is to analyze her device and determine whether or not this invention is a good idea to release to the public.

[figure] A horizontal rectangular duct labeled "Heat exchanger" with state 1 at the left inlet (a short vertical tick labeled 1) and state 2 at the right exit (a short vertical tick labeled 2). At the exit of the duct on the right, four diverging straight lines fan out to the right, labeled "Nozzles" above them, representing four identical nozzles discharging the air.

[handwritten] ②

a. The specifications on his patent application show that the working fluid should be air and the mass flow at the entrance to the heat exchanger is a steady 8 kg/s. Redraw a sketch of the device with a control volume, showing where the mass and energy flows are, and calculate the mass flow through the end of each nozzle.

[handwritten] ③

b. Air is sucked into the heat exchanger at 300 K and 0.2 MPa. 1 kg/s of water enters the heat exchanger as a subcooled liquid with a temperature of 500 K and a pressure of 10 MPa and leaves at the same pressure and a temperature of 380 K. Given these conditions, what is the temperature of the air leaving the heat exchanger? Make sure to list your assumptions.

[handwritten] Given : air
[handwritten] ② $\dot{m}_a = 8\ \text{kg/s}$
[handwritten] $T_1 = 300\ \text{K}$
[handwritten] $P_1 = 0.2\ \text{MPa}$

[handwritten] water
[handwritten] $\dot{m} = 1\ \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{s}}$
[handwritten] $T_1 = 500\ \text{K}$
[handwritten] $P_1 = 10\ \text{MPa}$
[handwritten] $P_2 = P_1$
[handwritten] $T_2 = 380\ \text{K}$

[handwritten] Assume : air is ideal
[handwritten] ① : H₂O is S.C.S. [?, likely "S.C.S." = saturated/compressed state — uncertain reading]
[handwritten] : quasi-steady
[handwritten] : $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = 0$
[handwritten] : passive $\rightarrow \dot{W} = 0$
[handwritten] : no heat lost to outside

[handwritten] Find : $T_{2,\text{air}}$ (underlined)

## Page 2

a)
[figure] A horizontal duct shown in cross-section. Air enters at the left with mass flow rate $\dot{m}$ (arrow pointing right into the duct). Inside the duct near the inlet, a curved arrow labeled $\dot{Q}$ indicates heat transfer (two curved arrows drawn). The duct expands at the right into four outlet nozzles, each discharging a stream labeled $\dot{m}/4$.

b) In air: $\dot{m}\left(\Delta h + \cancel{\Delta ke} + \cancel{\Delta pe}\right) = \dot{Q} - \cancel{\dot{W}}$

$\dot{m}\Delta h = \dot{Q}$

$\dot{m}c_p \Delta T = \dot{Q} \rightarrow T_2 = T_1 + \dfrac{\dot{Q}}{\dot{m}c_p}$ [handwritten] comes from water

In water: $\dot{m}\left(\Delta h + \cancel{\Delta ke} + \cancel{\Delta pe}\right) = \dot{Q} - \cancel{\dot{W}}$

$\dot{m}\Delta h = \dot{Q}$

$\rightarrow$ State 1: $T_1 = 500\ \text{K}$, $P_1 = 10\ \text{MPa}$ $\left.\begin{matrix}\\\\\end{matrix}\right\}$ [handwritten] saturated liquid, Table D.4B

$h_1 = 977.18\ \dfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}$

$\rightarrow$ State 2: $T_2 = 380\ \text{K}$, $P_2 = P_1 = 10\ \text{MPa}$ $\left.\begin{matrix}\\\\\end{matrix}\right\}$ [handwritten] Table D.4B

$h_2 = 458.37\ \dfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}$

$\Rightarrow \dot{Q}_{water} = -\dot{Q}_{air} = \dot{m}(h_2 - h_1) = (1)(458.37 - 977.18)$
$\phantom{\Rightarrow \dot{Q}_{water}} = -521.81\ \dfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{s}}$

$\Rightarrow \dot{Q}_{air} = 521.81\ \dfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{s}}$
