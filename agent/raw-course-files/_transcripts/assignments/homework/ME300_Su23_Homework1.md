**ME 300 Homework 1: Energy Conservation and Basic Principles**

Answer the following questions neatly on a separate piece of paper or on a tablet. Written answers can be typed. Show all your work and explain your reasoning – partial credit will only be given if you show and explain each of your steps. Submit your homework on Canvas.

1. (10 points) Please fill out the energy survey on Canvas. This is a survey to get a baseline of your understanding of energy concepts at the beginning of the class. Do not look up any of the answers online!!! You will get full credit for simply answering the questions, so just do your best.

2. (10 points) Again, without looking up the answers, explain how electricity is produced and gets to your appliances. Let's use your coffee pot as an example – work back from the plug of your coffee pot all the way to the original source of energy. Where does electricity come from and does it get to your coffee pot? Answer this question to the best of your ability without any resources, it will be graded on completion only.

3. (30 points) Consider these five conservation scenarios and provide the following information about how to solve for the scenario:
   - Indicate whether it's appropriate to use a finite-time or instantaneous conservation formulation.
   - Write the form of the conservation equation, cancelling any terms that are not required and explaining why these terms can be neglected.

   a. One way to conserve water is to catch it in a rain barrel to water your plants and your lawn (https://extension.psu.edu/rain-barrels-for-water-conservation). In this scenario, a rain bucket with a volume V collects rain at a rate of $\dot{m}_{rain}$ during a rain storm. Assume that the bucket doesn't overflow. Write a conservation of mass statement for the water in this scenario.

   b. You want to make coffee using a Mr. Coffee (classic), which heats the water in the well at a rate of $\dot{Q}_{heat}$, but the coffee machine loses heat at a rate of $\dot{Q}_{loss}$. Write a conservation of energy statement for the water in this scenario.

   c. Just as you're about to make more coffee, you find coffee still in the pot from the morning – bonus! You put it in a mug and put it in the microwave for 1 minute, where $Q_{in}$ heat is added to the cup. Write a conservation of energy statement for the coffee in this scenario.

   d. Natural gas is used in a wide range of applications, from power generation to home heating. In order to move natural gas around the country, there is a network of gas pipelines where gas flows from wells to local distribution networks. In order to keep the gas moving, you need to "recompress" the gas along the pipeline
