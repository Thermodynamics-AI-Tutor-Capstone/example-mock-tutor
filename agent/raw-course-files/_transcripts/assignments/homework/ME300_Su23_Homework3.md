**ME 300 Homework 3: Assumptions, Properties, and Ideal Gases**

Answer the following questions neatly on a separate piece of paper or on a tablet. Written answers can be typed. Show all your work and explain your reasoning – partial credit will only be given if you show and explain each of your steps. Submit your homework on Canvas.

1. (20 points) A sealed cylindrical vessel has the inner dimensions of 58.2 inches tall and 9 inches in diameter. The vessel holds 25 lb of nitrogen (N₂) at 2900 psi.
   - Convert these specifications into SI units. What is the volume of the vessel in SI units?
   - How many moles of nitrogen are in the vessel?
   - What is the particular gas constant of nitrogen?
   - Assuming nitrogen is an ideal gas, what is the temperature of the nitrogen in the vessel?
   - Now I replace the nitrogen with the same number of moles of Argon at the same pressure. What is the temperature in the vessel now?

2. (10 points) A pressure transducer is hooked up to the vessel with N₂ (not N) in Problem #1, and the transducer reads gage pressure. Assume that the pressure from Problem #1 is the absolute pressure.
   - What is the gage pressure read by the transducer in SI units if the vessel is located in Washington DC (sea level)?
   - What happens to the gage pressure if I now take the vessel to Denver, CO?

3. (20 points) Below is a table of molecular weights of the most common substances we'll be using in this class. Use these molecular weights to answer the following questions.

| Species | Molecular weight |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N₂) | 28.0134 g/mol |
| Oxygen (O₂) | 31.9988 g/mol |
| Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | 44.01 g/mol |

   a. What is the particular gas constant for O₂?
   b. The mass-specific volume of a system of CO₂ is 10 m³/kg – what is its molar-specific volume?
   c. Which substance has a particular gas constant of 188.92 J/kg-K?
   d. A gas cylinder has 20 kg of H₂O in it – how many moles of H₂O are in the cylinder?
   e. The container has molar-specific volume of N₂ of 0.1 m³/mol – what is the density of this nitrogen?
