## Page 2
### Second Law: First Statement (1A)

[handwritten]
→ Although work can be completely converted to heat, heat cannot be completely and continuously converted to work. ("completely" and "continuously" underlined)

Arrow from "completely":
- heat → $\Delta T$
- $T$ → mean velocity of molecules

work = force × distance
→ directional

## Page 3
### Second Law: First Statement (1B)

[handwritten]
Kelvin-Planck: It is impossible to construct a cyclically-operating device for which the sole effect is the exchange of heat from a single reservoir and creation of the equivalent amount of work. ("cyclically-operating" and "single reservoir" underlined)

→ cyclically operating = continuously
→ reservoir = source/sink of heat with a constant temp. ("sink" written under "source")

$$\dot{Q}_{in} \rightarrow \boxed{\text{cycle}} \rightarrow \dot{W} \neq \dot{Q}_{in}$$

### Does not apply to processes

[figure][handwritten] Same piston–cylinder sketch: $W_{out}$ up from the piston, $Q_{in}$ into the bottom.

[handwritten]
isothermal → $\Delta T = 0$
ideal gas → $\Delta u = c_v \Delta T = 0$
$\Rightarrow \Delta KE = \Delta PE = 0$
$$\Rightarrow \Delta U = Q - W = 0 \quad \Rightarrow \text{process: } Q = W$$
★ does **not** violate 2nd law
