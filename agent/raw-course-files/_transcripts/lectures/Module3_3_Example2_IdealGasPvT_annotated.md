## Page 2
Example: Ideal gas

Problem:
- I have two containers with the same volume and at the same temperature inside. One has 10 grams of Helium in one and 10 grams of Argon in the other. Which one has a higher pressure?
- Molecular weights:
  - Ar = 40 kg/kmol
  - He = 4 kg/kmol

[handwritten] A bracket around the molecular weights labelled $\mathcal{M}$ (script M = molar mass).

[handwritten]
$$T_{He} = T_{Ar}$$
$$V_{He} = V_{Ar}$$ (script V = total volume)
$$M_{He} = M_{Ar}$$

## Page 3
Which vessel has a higher pressure?

a) Helium
b) Argon

[figure] Speech-bubble with question mark on yellow background. (No handwritten answer on this page.)

## Page 4
Method #1

[handwritten]
$$PV = NR_uT$$ with arrow to: $N \sim [\text{mol}]$, $R_u = 8.314 \ \frac{\text{J}}{\text{mol-K}}$

$$V_{He} = V_{Ar}$$
$$T_{He} = T_{Ar}$$
$$P = \frac{NR_uT}{V} \Rightarrow N?$$

Argon: $$N_{Ar} = \frac{M_{Ar}}{\mathcal{M}_{Ar}} = \frac{10 \text{ kg}}{40 \text{ kg/kmol}} = 0.25 \text{ kmol}$$

Helium: $$N_{He} = \frac{M_{He}}{\mathcal{M}_{He}} = \frac{10 \text{ kg}}{4 \text{ kg/kmol}} = 2.5 \text{ kmol}$$

**ANSWER:** (boxed) $P_{He} = 10\, P_{Ar}$

[transcriber note: the problem statement says 10 grams, but the handwritten work uses 10 kg; the mole numbers would be 0.25 mol and 2.5 mol for 10 g. The ratio $P_{He} = 10 P_{Ar}$ is unaffected.]

## Page 5
Method #2

[handwritten]
$$PV = MRT$$ (check marks over $V$, $M$, $T$ — equal for both gases)

↳ $$R_{Ar} = \frac{R_u}{\mathcal{M}_{Ar}} = 208 \ \frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg-K}}$$
$$R_{He} = \frac{R_u}{\mathcal{M}_{He}} = 2080 \ \frac{\text{J}}{\text{kg-K}}$$

(brace) $$P = \frac{MRT}{V}$$ (check marks over $M$, $T$)
$$P_{He} > P_{Ar}$$
$$R_{He} > R_{Ar}$$

**ANSWER:** a) Helium (from $P_{He} > P_{Ar}$)
