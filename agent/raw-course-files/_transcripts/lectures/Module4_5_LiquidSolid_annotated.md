## Page 2
Liquids

[handwritten]
↳ incompressible: $v(T,P) \approx v(T)$        ⇒ $h(T,P) = h(T,P)$
$u(T,P) \approx u(T)$

⇒ $v(T,P) \approx v_f(T = T_{sat})$
$u(T,P) \approx u_f(T = T_{sat})$
$h(T,P) \approx u_f(T = T_{sat}) + (P - P_{sat})\, v(T = T_{sat})$
(arrow pointing to $P_{sat}$ with note: "at T")

[transcriber note: the enthalpy line reads "$u_f$" as the first term as far as legible; the usual textbook compressed-liquid approximation is $h \approx h_f(T) + v_f(T)\,(P - P_{sat}(T))$, equivalently $u_f + P v_f$. Transcribed as written. The "$h(T,P) = h(T,P)$" at top right is also as written — presumably meaning h keeps its P dependence.]

[figure] Hand-sketched T–v diagram: vapor dome, with two constant-pressure lines each rising steeply along the compressed-liquid side (nearly coinciding with the saturated-liquid line), running horizontally across the dome, then rising in the vapor region. Illustrates that compressed-liquid states lie almost on the saturated-liquid line.
