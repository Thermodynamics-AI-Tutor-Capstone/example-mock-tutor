## Page 5
Ideal gas – P-v-T equation of state

[handwritten] Ideal gas: (boxed, double-underlined) $$Pv = RT$$
$P \sim [\text{Pa}]$, $T \sim [\text{K}]$, $v \sim [\text{m}^3/\text{kg}]$

[handwritten] $$R = \frac{R_u}{\mathcal{M}}$$ with arrow: "particular gas const."
$$R_u = 8314.472 \ \frac{\text{J}}{\text{kmol-K}}$$
$$\mathcal{M} \sim [\text{kg/kmol}]$$ (script M = molar mass; units underlined)

[handwritten]
$$P = \rho R T$$
$$PV = MRT \to V \sim [\text{m}^3]$$ (script V = total volume)
$$PV = NR_uT \to N \sim [\text{mol}]$$
(a bracket joins the two lines above) ⇒ $$\mathcal{M} = \frac{M}{N}$$

[handwritten] (boxed, double-underlined) $$P\bar{v} = R_uT \to \bar{v} \sim [\text{m}^3/\text{mol}]$$

## Page 6
Ideal gas – Assumptions to derive P-v-T EOS

[handwritten] $$P\bar{v} = R_uT \to PV = NR_uT$$

[handwritten] (bracketed, with a star in the margin)
1. molecules are far apart
   → $V_{mol} \lll V_{b/w\ molecules}$
   → collisions are infrequent
2. collisions are elastic
   → intermolecular forces are negligible
