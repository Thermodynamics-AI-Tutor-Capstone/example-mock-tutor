## Problem

The concept of quasi-equilibrium is one we'll use on every single analysis throughout the semester. In this problem, we're going to extend our analysis from class on the large ship engines to car engines to determine whether a process can be considered quasi-equilibrium. Mazda is a company that has been extremely innovative in the internal combustion engine space, working to get the most power and highest efficiency of this technology. Their SkyActiv technology is a package that improves engine efficiency significantly while still making the car a lot of fun to drive – do some research online to learn how they do it. In this problem, let's consider the Mazda 3 SkyActiv (hatchbacks for the win) – [https://www.mazdausa.com/vehicles/2022-mazda3-hatchback](https://www.mazdausa.com/vehicles/2022-mazda3-hatchback).

a. First, explain what the quasi-equilibrium assumption is and why it's important.

b. Next, use the Mazda website to find the bore (width of the cylinder), stroke (length the piston travels over half a rotation), and maximum engine speed (referred to as "redline" on the website) of the engine. Use this information to calculate the timescale of the compression stroke at a highway driving engine speed of 2000 RPM.

c. Use Excel or Matlab to plot the timescale of a compression stroke as a function of engine speed from 0 to the maximum engine speed.

d. At what engine speed would the process no longer be quasi-equilibrium? Why?

## What it tests

- Students can explain the quasi-equilibrium assumption and its importance.
- Students can find engine specifications from a manufacturer website.
- Students can calculate the compression stroke timescale from engine speed.
- Students can plot timescale as a function of engine speed.
- Students can estimate the engine speed at which quasi-equilibrium breaks down using a sound-speed limit.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Compression stroke time is half of a revolution.
- The limiting condition is when piston speed equals the speed of sound.
- Speed of sound is taken as $340\ \text{m/s}$.
- Bore = 89 mm, stroke = 100 mm, redline = 6500 RPM from the Mazda website.

## Where students go wrong

- Defining quasi-equilibrium merely as 'slow' without comparing process timescale to system equilibration timescale.
- Using the full revolution period instead of half a revolution for the compression stroke.
- Confusing revolutions per minute with revolutions per second.
- Plotting against the wrong independent variable or not including the range from 0 to redline.
- Using $v_{\text{piston}} = 2L\,\text{RPM}$ instead of $v_{\text{piston}} = L/\tau_{\text{stroke}}$.
- Forgetting the factor of 2 in $\text{RPM} = c_{\text{sound}}/(2L)$.
- Not converting the final cycle rate to revolutions per minute.

## How it is graded

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Quasi-equilibrium assumption: The process occurs slowly enough such that the system equilibrates much more quickly (on a shorter timescale) than the speed of the process (longer timescale).
- Bore: 89 mm
- Stroke: 100 mm
- Maximum engine speed (redline): 6500 RPM
- Timescale of compression stroke at 2000 RPM: 0.00025 s
- Plot formula: \tau_{\text{stroke}} = \frac{1}{2}\,\tau_{\text{revolution}} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\text{RPM}}\right)
- Piston speed limit criterion: v_{\text{piston}} = c_{\text{sound}}
- Limiting engine speed: 100000 RPM
