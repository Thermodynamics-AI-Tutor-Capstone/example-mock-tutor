# Exam 2 (Summer 2022), problem III.a-3e: Steam-plant boiler and turbine analysis

## Problem

The West Campus Steam Plant uses boilers to produce steam for heating campus. In this problem, some steam is expanded through a turbine to make power. The system has two components in series: a boiler (heat exchanger) followed by a turbine. Both components operate reversibly.

Given: mass flow rate dot m = 20 kg/s entering the boiler; heat input to boiler dot Q_in = 35,226 kW; boiler inlet state 1: P_1 = 6 MPa, x_1 = 0; boiler outlet state 2: P_2 = 6 MPa; turbine outlet state 3: P_3 = 1 MPa.

a) List the assumptions necessary to calculate the state points before and after the heat exchanger and the power generated by the turbine.

b) Calculate the temperature of the steam after the heat exchanger. Identify the table you used.

c) Calculate the temperature of the steam after the turbine. Identify the table you used.

d) Calculate the power produced by the turbine in [kW].

e) Draw this process on a T-s diagram, labeling the state points and phases of fluid at each state.

## What it tests

- Students can locate states in the steam tables using pressure and quality or enthalpy.
- Students can apply the steady-flow energy equation to a boiler/heat exchanger and a turbine.
- Students can relate turbine work to enthalpy change and entropy for a reversible process.
- Students can identify fluid phase and sketch the process on a T-s diagram with the saturation dome.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- steady-state steady-flow
- quasi-equilibrium
- steady flow
- Boiler: Delta ke = Delta pe = Wdot = 0
- Turbine: Delta ke = Delta pe = Qdot = 0
- reversible process

## Where students go wrong

- Not recognizing x_1 = 0 as saturated liquid and using h_g instead of h_f at state 1.
- In the boiler, writing h_2 = h_1 + Q instead of h_2 = h_1 + Qdot/mdot, forgetting mass flow rate.
- Looking in a saturated table for state 2 even though h_2 > h_g indicates superheated vapor; not switching to a superheated table.
- For the turbine, not setting s_3 = s_2 because the process is reversible; treating it as isothermal or isenthalpic.
- Not recognizing state 3 as a saturated mixture because s_f < s_3 < s_g at P_3.
- Miscomputing quality x_3 from s values, or using h_3 = h_f + x_3(h_g - h_f) incorrectly.
- Sign error in the turbine steady-flow equation, e.g. Wdot = +mdot(h_3 - h_2) instead of Wdot = -mdot(h_3 - h_2).
- Drawing the T-s diagram without the saturation dome or locating the phases incorrectly.

## How it is graded

Points were awarded for each assumption (1/2 each), the boiler energy balance and table lookups, the turbine entropy equality, quality calculation, enthalpy interpolation, final power, and T-s diagram features and labels.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Assumptions: steady-state steady-flow; quasi-equilibrium; boiler has negligible kinetic/potential energy changes and no shaft work; turbine has negligible kinetic/potential energy changes and no heat transfer; reversible
- $T_2$: 600 K
- $T_3$: 453.03 K
- $Wdot_turbine$: 7238 kW
- T-s diagram: Saturation dome with state 1 as saturated liquid at 6 MPa, state 2 as superheated vapor at 6 MPa, horizontal boiler path 1-2, vertical turbine path 2-3 to mixture at 1 MPa
