# Exam 2 (Summer 2021), problem II.1: Isentropic compression of steam and air

## Problem

Steam is compressed isentropically from a saturated vapor at 2.1 MPa to a pressure of 6 MPa. Identify any tables you use at each step of the problem. If you use a superheated vapor or subcooled liquid table, do not interpolate between values; use the closest value on the table. (a) Draw this process on a T-s diagram. (b) What is the temperature at the end of this compression? (c) What would the final temperature be if it was air being compressed from the same starting temperature and pressure to the same final pressure? Assume the following properties for air: $c_p=1000$ J/kg-K and $c_v=714.29$ J/kg-K.

## What it tests

- Students can locate states in steam tables for an isentropic compression process.
- Students can draw an isentropic compression on a T-s diagram inside the superheated region.
- Students can apply the ideal-gas isentropic relation for air with constant specific heats.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Steam is a pure substance; use Tables D.2 and D.3.
- No interpolation in superheated or subcooled tables; use the closest table value.
- The process is isentropic, so $s_2 = s_1$.
- For part (c), air is an ideal gas with constant specific heats, so the isentropic ideal-gas relation applies.

## Where students go wrong

- Using the ideal-gas isentropic relation for the steam part instead of steam tables.
- Not recognizing state 1 is on the saturated vapor line and reading $s_1$ from the saturated steam table at $P_1=2.1$ MPa.
- Treating state 2 as saturated vapor at 6 MPa instead of superheated vapor with $s_2=s_1$.
- Interpolating in the superheated table even though the problem forbids interpolation.
- Computing $k$ incorrectly for air; $k=c_p/c_v=1000/714.29=1.4$.
- Using $P_1$ or $P_2$ in inconsistent pressure units in the pressure ratio.
- Drawing the T-s diagram with state 1 in the compressed-liquid region, or not showing the vertical constant-entropy line from state 1 to state 2.

## How it is graded

T-s diagram earns 4 pts; part (b) rewards table selection, reading $s_1$, setting $s_2=s_1$, and reading $T_2$; part (c) rewards the isentropic ideal-gas relation and final temperature.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $T_2$ for steam$: 620 K
- $T_2$ for air$: 658.7 K
