# Homework 5 (Summer 2023), problem 4: Isobaric condensation of saturated vapor to saturated liquid

## Problem

4. (20 points) Initially, 1 kg of H$_2$O at 2 MPa is a saturated vapor. The water is slowly compressed in an isobaric process until it is entirely saturated liquid.
a. Sketch the process on P-v and T-v plots. Label the initial and final states.
b. Determine the change in temperature of the fluid. Explain your answer.
c. Determine the work interaction (J). Is the work "in" or "out" of the system?
d. Determine the heat interaction (J). Is the heat "in" or "out" of the system?

## What it tests

- Students can sketch a constant-pressure condensation process on $P-v$ and $T-v$ diagrams
- Students can use saturated water tables to find $T_{sat}$, $v_f$, $v_g$, $u_f$, and $u_g$
- Students can compute moving-boundary work during a two-phase isobaric process
- Students can apply the first law to determine heat transfer and identify work/heat direction

## Assumptions the solution expects

- S.C.S.
- Quasi-equilibrium
- Negligible kinetic and potential energy changes, $\Delta KE=\Delta PE=0$

## Where students go wrong

- Expecting the temperature to change during the isobaric phase change instead of recognizing it remains at $T_{sat}$
- Using superheated or compressed-liquid tables instead of saturated water tables at 2 MPa
- Reversing $v_f-v_g$ and obtaining positive work for a compression
- Forgetting to multiply the specific-volume difference by mass, or using total volume instead of specific volume
- Not converting MPa to Pa when computing work in Joules
- Mislabeling the sign of the heat transfer: negative heat means heat is leaving the system

## How it is graded

Points reward correct sketches with labeled initial and final states, $T_{sat}=485.53$ K with $\Delta T=0$, the constant-pressure work $MP(v_f-v_g)$, and the first-law calculation for heat transfer with correct signs.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $T_1=T_2=T_{sat}$: 485.53 K
- $\Delta T$: 0 K
- ${}_1\bar{W}_2$: -196816.6 J
- Work direction: In (done on the fluid)
- ${}_1Q_2$: -1889.777 kJ
- Heat direction: Out (removed from the fluid)
