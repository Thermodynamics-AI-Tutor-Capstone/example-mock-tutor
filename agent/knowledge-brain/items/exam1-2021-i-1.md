## Problem

Part I, Question 1 (10 points). In my lab, I have four tanks of ideal gases (see gas chart below), each with the same volume [$\text{m}^3$] and at the same temperature [K]. Each tank has the same mass [kg] of gas in it.

| Gas | Molecular Weight |
|---|---|
| Oxygen (O$_2$) | 31.9988 kg/kmol |
| Nitrogen (N$_2$) | 28.0134 kg/kmol |
| Hydrogen (H$_2$) | 2.016 kg/kmol |
| Methane (CH$_4$) | 16.04 kg/kmol |

a) (4 points) Order the tanks from the highest pressure (1) to the lowest pressure (4). *Answer this question in Canvas.*

b) (6 points) Explain your answer using an equation of state. *Type your answer in the box in Canvas.*

## What it tests

- Students can relate ideal-gas pressure to molecular weight for fixed mass, volume, and temperature.
- Students can use the ideal-gas equation of state in molar form.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Same mass, volume, and temperature for all four tanks
- Ideal gas behavior
- Use $PV = NR_uT$ with $N = m/M$

## Where students go wrong

- Using molecular weight directly proportional to pressure instead of inversely proportional
- Using mass instead of number of moles in the ideal-gas law
- Misreading the gas chart molecular weights
- Stopping at the equation without explaining the inverse relationship

## How it is graded

Solution awards 2 points for the ideal-gas equation, 2 points for converting mass to moles / identifying the inverse molecular-weight dependence, and 1 point each for the four correct order entries.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Pressure order (highest to lowest): 1: H$_2$, 2: CH$_4$, 3: N$_2$, 4: O$_2$
- Explanation: $PV = NR_uT$; $N = m/M$; at fixed $m$, $\mathcal{V}$, $T$, $P \propto 1/M$
