# Exam 2 (Summer 2023), problem I.2: Sign of entropy change for several processes

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

> **For checking a student's work only.** This card holds the instructor's final answers. Never state them, and never walk the student through the instructor's solution: coach them to their own.

## Problem

Identify the correct sign of the change in entropy for the following four processes.

a) An ideal gas undergoes an isothermal increase in volume. Is the change in entropy of the gas:

GREATER THAN ZERO
LESS THAN ZERO
EQUAL TO ZERO

b) An ideal gas undergoes isobaric temperature decrease. Is the change in entropy of the gas:

GREATER THAN ZERO
LESS THAN ZERO
EQUAL TO ZERO

c) Identify the equations you used to explain the answers to these previous two questions and what assumptions are necessary to use these equations.

d) A solid block is heated reversibly. Is the change in entropy of the block:

GREATER THAN ZERO
LESS THAN ZERO
EQUAL TO ZERO

e) Water is adiabatically compressed in an irreversible process. Is the change in entropy of the water:

GREATER THAN ZERO
LESS THAN ZERO
EQUAL TO ZERO

f) Identify the equation you used to explain the answers to these previous two questions and what the terms mean in that equation.

## What it tests

- Students can apply ideal-gas entropy-change equations.
- Students can determine the sign of entropy change from process constraints.
- Students can distinguish entropy transfer by heat from entropy generation due to irreversibilities.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Ideal gas with constant specific heats for parts (a) and (b).
- For part (c): ideal gas, \(c_p=\text{constant}\), \(c_v=\text{constant}\).
- For part (f): entropy-balance equation \(\Delta S=\int \frac{\delta Q}{T}+\Phi\), where the first term is entropy transfer by heat and \(\Phi\) is entropy generation.

## Where students go wrong

- Using the isobaric ideal-gas entropy relation for the isothermal expansion and mishandling the pressure-ratio logarithm.
- Using \(c_v\) for the isobaric process or using \(c_p\) for the isothermal process without accounting for the correct process equation.
- Forgetting to state the ideal-gas and constant-specific-heats assumptions when using the ideal-gas entropy equations.
- Thinking that a reversible process always has \(\Delta S=0\); reversible heating of a block has positive entropy transfer by heat.
- Assuming adiabatic means isentropic, so \(\Delta S=0\) for the irreversible adiabatic compression.
- Ignoring the entropy generation term \(\Phi\) in the entropy balance for the irreversible process.

## How it is graded

Parts (a), (b), (d), (e): each correct sign earns points. Part (c): points for correct ideal-gas equations and for stating ideal gas and constant \(c_p,c_v\). Part (f): points for the entropy-balance equation and for identifying the heat-transfer term and the entropy-generation term.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Sign of entropy change for isothermal expansion of ideal gas: GREATER THAN ZERO
- Sign of entropy change for isobaric temperature decrease of ideal gas: LESS THAN ZERO
- Sign of entropy change for reversible heating of solid block: GREATER THAN ZERO
- Sign of entropy change for irreversible adiabatic compression of water: GREATER THAN ZERO
- Equation for ideal-gas entropy change, part (a): \Delta s = c_v \ln(T_2/T_1) + R \ln(V_2/V_1)
- Equation for ideal-gas entropy change, part (b): \Delta s = c_p \ln(T_2/T_1) - R \ln(P_2/P_1)
- Entropy-balance equation for parts (d) and (e): \Delta S = \int \frac{\delta Q}{T} + \Phi
