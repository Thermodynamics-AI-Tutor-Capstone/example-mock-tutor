## Problem

The Taurus 60 gas turbine in the West Campus Steam Plant has a compressor pressure ratio of 12.2 and a mass flow rate of 21.5 kg/s. The combustor adds 18 MW of heat to the cycle, and the isentropic efficiency of the compressor and turbine are 0.85 and 0.9, respectively. Assuming an inlet condition of $T_1=278$ K and $P_1=95,900$ Pa to do an air-standard cycle analysis to calculate the following. Use $c_p=1001$ J/kg-K and $\gamma=1.4$.

a) Temperature and pressure at all four state points

b) Power produced by the engine

c) Engine thermal efficiency

## What it tests

- Students can evaluate an air-standard Brayton cycle with non-ideal compressor and turbine.
- Students can calculate state temperatures and pressures using isentropic relations and efficiencies.
- Students can compute net power from compressor and turbine work.
- Students can compute engine thermal efficiency.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- air-standard cycle
- quasi-equilibrium
- steady-flow
- negligible-kinetic-energy and negligible-potential-energy
- compressor and turbine: adiabatic, no heat transfer
- combustor: no work
- ideal gas with constant specific heats

## Where students go wrong

- Using $T_2 = T_1(P_2/P_1)$ without the $(\gamma-1)/\gamma$ exponent.
- Reversing isentropic efficiency for the compressor or turbine, for example using $\eta_{comp}=(T_2-T_1)/(T_{2s}-T_1)$ instead of the ideal-to-real ratio.
- Forgetting that $P_3=P_2$ across the combustor or $P_4=P_1$ at the turbine exhaust.
- Adding compressor and turbine work with inconsistent signs; the compressor absorbs work while the turbine produces work.
- Unit errors: using heat addition in W instead of MW or mismatching $c_p$, $\dot{m}$, and power units.
- The solution transcript has a compressor-work line showing $T_1=298$ K instead of the given 278 K; using 298 K will not reproduce the boxed net work.

## How it is graded

Solution points reward assumptions, compressor pressure and isentropic temperature, compressor efficiency, real compressor temperature, combustor energy balance, turbine isentropic and real temperatures, net work, and thermal efficiency.
