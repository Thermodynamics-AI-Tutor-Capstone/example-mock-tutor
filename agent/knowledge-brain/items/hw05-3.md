# Homework 5 (Summer 2023), problem 3: Ideal gas in a locked piston: isochoric then isobaric process

## Problem

3. (30 points) Air ($M=5$ kg) is trapped in a sealed piston system with a volume of $V_1=1$ m$^3$ that is initially locked so that the piston can't move. The air is heated from $T_1=300$ K to $T_2=500$ K. At this point, the lock breaks, the heater goes haywire, and the piston rises in an isobaric process until the final volume of $V_3=3$ m$^3$ is reached. Calculate the following knowing that $R=287$ J/kg-K and $c_v=714$ J/kg-K:
a. The pressure, temperature, and volume of the gas at all three states
b. The change in internal energy (units of Joules) of the gas for these two processes
c. The work (units of Joules) done for these two processes - was work done "by" or "on" the fluid?
d. The heat transfer (units of Joules) for these two processes.

## What it tests

- Students can use the ideal gas law to determine pressure, temperature, and volume at each state
- Students can compute internal energy changes using $M c_v \Delta T$
- Students can compute moving-boundary work for constant-volume and constant-pressure processes
- Students can apply the first law with correct sign conventions for work and heat

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Closed system (sealed piston)
- Ideal gas
- Constant specific heat, $c_v=$ constant
- Quasi-equilibrium
- Process 1-2 is isochoric because the piston is locked, so $V_2=V_1$
- Process 2-3 is isobaric, so $P_3=P_2$
- Negligible kinetic and potential energy changes, $\Delta KE=0$, $\Delta PE=0$

## Where students go wrong

- Using specific volume instead of total volume in the ideal-gas law, or forgetting to multiply by mass
- Using $c_p$ or $R$ in place of $c_v$ when computing internal energy change
- Allowing volume to change during process 1-2 even though the piston is locked
- Using $P_1$ or an average pressure for the constant-pressure work from state 2 to state 3 instead of $P_2=P_3=717500$ Pa
- Interpreting positive expansion work as work done on the fluid
- Using the wrong sign convention in the first law, for example computing ${}_2Q_3$ as $\Delta U - W$ instead of $\Delta U + W$

## How it is graded

Points reward listing assumptions, computing each state with the ideal gas law, applying $\Delta U = M c_v \Delta T$, recognizing zero work for constant volume, computing $P(V_3-V_2)$ for the constant-pressure work, and applying the first law to obtain heat transfers.
