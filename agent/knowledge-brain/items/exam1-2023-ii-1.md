## Problem

The following table gives a read-out from NIST for an unknown species. The molecular weight of this substance is 34.08 kg/kmol and the universal gas constant is 8314.5 J/kmol-K.

| Temperature (K) | Pressure (MPa) | Density (kg/m^3) | Volume (m^3/kg) | Internal Energy (kJ/kg) | Enthalpy (kJ/kg) | Entropy (J/g*K) | Cv (J/g*K) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 300.00 | 0.50000 | 7.1019 | 0.14081 | 557.47 | 627.87 | 2.3101 | 0.78245 |
| 350.00 | 0.50000 | 5.9938 | 0.16684 | 597.55 | 680.97 | 2.6739 | 0.78900 |
| 400.00 | 0.50000 | 5.2013 | 0.19226 | 637.92 | 734.05 | 2.8156 | 0.80574 |
| 450.00 | 0.50000 | 4.6007 | 0.21736 | 679.09 | 787.77 | 2.9421 | 0.82714 |
| 500.00 | 0.50000 | 4.1277 | 0.24227 | 721.30 | 842.43 | 3.0573 | 0.85095 |

Critical temperature $T_c = 373.1$ K and critical pressure $P_c = 9.0000$ MPa.

a) Is this species an ideal gas or not at the range of pressures and temperatures shown in the table?
b) Use an equation of state to explain why this species is an ideal gas or not.
c) Use the critical temperature and pressure to explain why this species is an ideal gas or not.

## What it tests

- Students can compute the gas constant from molecular weight.
- Students can test ideal-gas behavior using $Pv=RT$ and the compressibility factor.
- Students can apply critical-property criteria for ideal-gas behavior.

## Assumptions the solution expects



## Where students go wrong

- Using density instead of specific volume directly in $Pv=RT$ without taking the reciprocal.
- Unit conversion errors between MPa and Pa or kmol and kg.
- Computing $Z$ but not comparing it to 1.
- Using only $T>T_c$ or only $P \ll P_c$ rather than both conditions.
- Using $c_v$ values incorrectly when checking ideal-gas behavior.

## How it is graded

Part a +4. Part b awards +4 for $Pv=RT$ approach, +1 for computing $R$, and +2 for the $Z$ values shown. Part c awards +2 for $T>T_c$, +2 for $P \ll P_c$, and +4 for the explanation that the molar volume is close to the ideal-gas value.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Part a: ideal gas or not: ideal gas
- Part b: species gas constant: 243.97 J/kg-K
- Part b: compressibility factor at 300 K, 0.5 MPa: 0.962
- Part b: compressibility factor at 500 K, 0.5 MPa: 0.993
- Part c: ideal-gas critical-property conditions: $T > T_c$ and $P \ll P_c$
