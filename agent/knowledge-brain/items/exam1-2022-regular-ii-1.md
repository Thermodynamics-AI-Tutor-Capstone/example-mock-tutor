# Exam 1 (Summer 2022), problem Regular II.1: Isobaric and isothermal expansion of air in a piston-cylinder

## Problem

An inventor is testing out different processes for air in a piston-cylinder arrangement from the same initial state. In both processes, the mass-specific volume of the chamber increases from $v_1=5$ m$^3$/kg to $v_2=10$ m$^3$/kg, but Process A is isobaric and Process B is isothermal. The temperature at state 1 is $T_1=500$ K. For air, $R=287$ J/kg-K and $c_v=714$ J/kg-K.

a) List the assumptions necessary to solve for the state points and work.

b) Draw both processes on the same P-v diagram and label the state points – State 1, State 2A, and State 2B.

c) Calculate $T_2$ and $P_2$ for both processes.

d) Calculate the work of the isobaric process in kilojoules [kJ] if the mass inside the cylinder is 1 kg.

e) Calculate the work of the isothermal process in kilojoules [kJ] if the mass inside the cylinder is 1 kg.

## What it tests

- Students can apply the ideal-gas law to find end states for isobaric and isothermal processes.
- Students can compute boundary work for constant-pressure and isothermal processes.
- Students can state kinematic and substance assumptions for piston-cylinder analysis.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Air is an ideal gas.
- $c_v$ is constant.
- Quasi-equilibrium process.
- $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = 0$.

## Where students go wrong

- Using $c_v$ instead of $R$ in the ideal-gas equation of state.
- Forgetting to multiply specific-volume change by mass in boundary work.
- Using natural log of pressure ratio instead of volume ratio for isothermal work.
- Not converting Pa m$^3$ to kJ correctly.
- Mislabeling Process A and Process B on the P-v diagram.

## How it is graded

Assumptions 2 pts total; P-v diagram 4 pts; state calculations 6 pts; isobaric work 3 pts; isothermal work 5 pts. Points for ideal-gas relation, process relations, and integration.
