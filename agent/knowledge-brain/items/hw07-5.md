## Problem

Explain the concept of thermal efficiency in your own words. Explain why having a higher thermal efficiency in your car engine results in lower CO2 emissions for the same amount of power delivered to the car. Do online research (cite sources) if you need to. (p. 2)

## What it tests

- Students can define thermal efficiency as useful work out divided by heat input.
- Students can relate higher thermal efficiency to lower fuel consumption for a fixed power output.
- Students can connect reduced fuel consumption to reduced CO2 emissions.

## Assumptions the solution expects



## Where students go wrong

- Defining efficiency as output over input without specifying useful work out vs. heat in.
- Claiming higher thermal efficiency increases power or heat, rather than reducing heat input for the same power.
- Missing the connection: less fuel burned per unit power means lower CO2.
- Ignoring that $\dot{Q}_{in}$ is supplied by combustion of fuel that produces CO2.

## How it is graded

+5 for thermal efficiency definition; +2 for $\dot{W} = \eta_{th}\dot{Q}_{in}$ and $\dot{Q}_{in}$ from combustion; +3 for explaining that a higher efficiency reduces $\dot{Q}_{in}$, fuel use, and CO2 for fixed power.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Thermal efficiency and CO2 link: $\eta_{th} = \frac{\text{what you get out}}{\text{heat you put in}}$; $\dot{W} = \eta_{th}\dot{Q}_{in}$, where $\dot{Q}_{in}$ comes from exothermic combustion: fuel + air → CO₂ + H₂O + heat. For fixed $\dot{W}$, increasing $\eta_{th}$ reduces $\dot{Q}_{in}$, so less fuel is burned and CO₂ emissions decrease.
