# Exam 2 (Summer 2023), problem I.1: Can thermal efficiency of a heat engine reach one?

## Problem

Can the thermal efficiency of a heat engine ever reach one? Answer the multiple-choice question in Canvas.

YES
NO

Explain your response to this question.

## What it tests

- Students can state the Kelvin-Planck statement of the second law.
- Students can relate thermal efficiency to net work and heat input.
- Students can justify why \(\eta_{th}<1\) for a heat engine.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- No heat engine can continuously and completely convert heat into work (Kelvin-Planck statement).

## Where students go wrong

- Saying YES because a reversible engine has no friction or losses; a reversible heat engine still rejects heat.
- Claiming \(\eta=1-\frac{Q_L}{Q_H}\) and setting \(Q_L=0\) without recognizing that this violates the Kelvin-Planck statement.
- Writing \(\eta=1-\frac{T_L}{T_H}\) and arguing that \(T_L=0\) is achievable.
- Stating \(\eta_{th}<1\) but not supporting it with Kelvin-Planck or with \(\dot{Q}_{in}>\dot{W}_{net}\).

## How it is graded

1 pt for NO; 1 pt for Kelvin-Planck statement; 1 pt for correct efficiency equation; 1 pt for concluding \(\eta_{th}<1\).

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Can thermal efficiency of a heat engine reach one: NO
