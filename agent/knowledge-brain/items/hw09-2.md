# Homework 9 (Summer 2023), problem 2: Compressor isentropic efficiency from measured plant data

## Problem

Use the data provided from the West Campus Steam Plant at the University Park campus. The data show the pressure and temperature at the inlet ($P_1$, $T_1$) and outlet ($P_2$, $T_2$) of the compressor of the gas turbine. Use the data to calculate the isentropic efficiency of the compressor at each hour of the data provided and plot the data vs. hour. Identify any assumptions you made and write out your calculation process before implementing it in Matlab or Excel. Discuss your final plot and what is happening to the isentropic efficiency with time. What sorts of things could the power plant operators do to increase the isentropic efficiency? (The actual data table is not included in the transcription.)

## What it tests

- Students can derive isentropic compressor efficiency in terms of temperatures for an ideal gas with constant $c_p$.
- Students can convert gage pressures to absolute pressures for compressor analysis.
- Students can interpret an efficiency-versus-time plot and propose operator actions to improve isentropic efficiency.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- ideal gas
- $c_p = \mathrm{const}$
- steady-flow
- $q=0$
- $\Delta ke \approx \Delta pe \approx 0$
- $P_2[\mathrm{psia}] = P_2[\mathrm{psig}] + P_1[\mathrm{psia}]$

## Where students go wrong

- Failing to convert outlet pressure from psig to psia before using the pressure ratio.
- Using the actual outlet temperature $T_2$ in the isentropic relation for $T_{2s}$ instead of computing $T_{2s}$ from the isentropic pressure-temperature relation.
- Using the wrong exponent, such as $k/(k-1)$ instead of $(k-1)/k$.
- Leaving $\dot{m}c_p$ in the work expression instead of canceling it in the efficiency formula.
- Assuming $q=0$ without stating it as an assumption.
- Inverting compressor efficiency as $\dot{W}_{real}/\dot{W}_{ideal}$ rather than $\dot{W}_{ideal}/\dot{W}_{real}$.
- Omitting the plot and discussion of efficiency versus hour or failing to suggest operator actions.

## How it is graded

Points reward stating assumptions and unit conversion, writing the compressor energy balance, using the ideal gas constant-$c_p$ relation, deriving the isentropic efficiency formula, plotting results, and discussing trends and operator actions.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Compressor isentropic efficiency: $\eta_{comp} = \frac{T_{2s}-T_1}{T_2-T_1}$, where $T_{2s}=T_1\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{(k-1)/k}$ dimensionless
- Gage-to-absolute pressure conversion: $P_2[\mathrm{psia}] = P_2[\mathrm{psig}] + P_1[\mathrm{psia}]$
