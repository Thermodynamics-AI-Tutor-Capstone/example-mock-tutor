## Problem

ME 300 Homework 4, Problem 1. The East Campus and West Campus Steam Plants are cogeneration plants; the engine is a gas turbine. Analyze air going through the compressor. Download the spreadsheet from Canvas that has temperature and pressure at the engine entrance ($T_1$, $P_1$) and after the compressor ($T_2$, $P_2$). Working fluid is air with $R = 287$ J/(kg K). Discuss the assumptions needed to calculate density, calculate density at both stations for each hour, plot both densities together versus time, and discuss trends.

## What it tests

- Students can apply the ideal-gas equation $\rho = P/(RT)$ to calculate density.
- Students can identify the unit conversions required when using $R = 287$ J/(kg K).
- Students can interpret time trends in density from measured temperature and pressure data.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- air behaves as an ideal gas
- temperature and pressure data plus the ideal-gas law are sufficient to calculate density

## Where students go wrong

- not stating the ideal-gas assumption needed for $\rho = P/(RT)$.
- using pressure in bar or MPa or temperature in Celsius directly with $R = 287$ J/(kg K).
- using the universal gas constant instead of the air-specific $R$.
- plotting $\rho_1$ or $\rho_2$ against something other than time or using separate scales.
- not explaining that higher daytime temperature lowers $\rho_1$ because pressure is nearly constant.

## How it is graded

Solution awards 2 points for the ideal-gas assumption, 3 points for the density relation, 4 points each for the two density plots, and 2 points for trend discussion.
