## Problem

Water begins in a saturated liquid state at 5.5 MPa and is isothermally heated until it is a saturated vapor.

a) Write the assumptions necessary to solve for State 2 and the heat and work.

b) What is the temperature of the water at State 1 in Kelvin [K]?

c) What is the temperature of the water at State 2 in Kelvin [K]?

d) What is the work done during this process in kilojoules [kJ] if the mass inside the cylinder is 1 kg?

e) What is the heat added to the system from State 1 to State 2 in kilojoules [kJ] if the mass inside the cylinder is 1 kg?

## What it tests

- Students can use saturated water tables to find temperature from pressure.
- Students can compute boundary work for an isobaric phase-change process.
- Students can use the first law to find heat from internal energy and work.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- S.C.S.
- Quasi-equilibrium process.
- $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = 0$.
- $x_1=0$ and $x_2=1$.

## Where students go wrong

- Using the wrong saturated vapor or saturated liquid specific volume.
- Forgetting to multiply specific-volume difference by mass.
- Using 5.5 MPa in the work calculation; the handwritten solution substitutes $5\times10^6$ Pa, and the transcript notes this inconsistency.
- Not converting MPa to Pa before computing work in J and then kJ.
- Reversing $u_g-u_f$ when computing heat.

## How it is graded

Assumptions 2 pts; T1 and T2 from Table D.2 2 pts each; work formula 4 pts plus answer 2 pts; first-law and internal-energy points for heat. Transcript notes the handwritten calculation uses $5\times10^6$ Pa although the given pressure is 5.5 MPa.
