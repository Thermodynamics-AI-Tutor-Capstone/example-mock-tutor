## Problem

Part II, Short Problem 2 (20 points). A rigid tank has a volume of 10 m$^3$ and holds 9.89 kg of water. The water is initially at 0.1 MPa, and is heated to 0.3 MPa. Identify any tables you use at each step of the problem.

a) Write the assumptions necessary to solve for State 2 and the heat and work. *Enter these in the Canvas quiz — you do not need to also write them on the page.*

b) What is the temperature of the water at State 1 in Kelvin [K]? *Enter this value in the Canvas quiz.*

c) What is the temperature of the water at State 2 in Kelvin [K]? *Enter this value in the Canvas quiz.*

d) What is the heat added to the system from State 1 to State 2 in kilojoules [kJ]? *Enter this value in the Canvas quiz.*

## What it tests

- Students can determine the phase region using specific volume and saturation tables.
- Students can evaluate internal energy in a two-phase mixture and a superheated vapor.
- Students can apply the first law to a rigid, sealed tank.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Closed system
- Rigid tank: $\Delta \mathcal{V} = 0$
- Sealed tank: $\Delta M = 0$
- Quasi-equilibrium process
- $\Delta KE = \Delta PE = 0$

## Where students go wrong

- Not recognizing the state is a two-phase mixture at State 1 because $v_f < v_1 < v_g$
- Using the saturated temperature for State 2 without checking that the vapor is superheated
- Overlooking that ${}_1W_2=0$ because the tank is rigid
- Miscalculating quality or internal energy at State 1
- Using Table D.2 instead of D.3F for superheated vapor at State 2

## How it is graded

Solution rewards assumptions, specific volume and region identification, State 1 and State 2 temperatures, first law with ${}_1W_2=0$, quality calculation, and final heat.
