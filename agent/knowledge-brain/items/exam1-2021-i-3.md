## Problem

Part I, Question 3 (10 points). Analyze the P-V diagram below for the given piston-cylinder system (also pictured below), which is similar to what would be seen in an automotive engine, to answer the following four questions.

a) (2 points) What type of process is this? *Answer the multiple-choice question in Canvas.*
A. Isothermal
B. Isobaric
C. Isochoric

b) (2 points) Which way does the piston move from State 1 to State 2? *Answer the multiple-choice question in Canvas.*
A. Up
B. Down

c) (2 points) What is the sign of work from State 1 to State 2? *Answer the multiple-choice question in Canvas.*
A. Positive
B. Negative

d) (4 points) Explain your answer to the previous question, "What is the sign of work from State 1 to State 2?" *Type your answer in the box in Canvas.*

## What it tests

- Students can identify a constant-pressure process on a P-v diagram.
- Students can relate volume change to piston motion.
- Students can determine the sign of boundary work from the integral of P dV.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Process is at constant pressure (isobaric)
- Pressure is positive
- Volume decreases from State 1 to State 2

## Where students go wrong

- Saying the work is positive because pressure is constant
- Confusing piston direction: decreasing volume can correspond to upward piston motion in this engine-like cylinder
- Identifying the process as isochoric or isothermal
- Forgetting the sign from the integration limits

## How it is graded

Multiple-choice answers awarded 2 points each; explanation awarded 2 points for writing the boundary-work integral and 2 points for the sign from $V_2 < V_1$.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Process type: Isobaric
- Piston motion from State 1 to State 2: Up
- Sign of work from State 1 to State 2: Negative
- Explanation: $V_2 < V_1$ and $P > 0$, so ${}_1W_2 = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} P\,dV < 0$
