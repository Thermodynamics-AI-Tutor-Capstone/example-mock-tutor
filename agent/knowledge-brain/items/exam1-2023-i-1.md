# Exam 1 (Summer 2023), problem I.1: Comparing isothermal and isochoric ideal-gas processes

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

## Problem

Two systems filled with the same mass of ideal gas start at the same state and then undergo two different processes from $P_1$ to $P_2$, where $P_2 < P_1$: an isothermal process (Process A) and an isochoric process (Process B).

a) Which process results in a higher volume at State 2?
b) Which process results in a higher change in internal energy from State 1 to State 2?
c) Explain your answer to part b.

## What it tests

- Students can interpret a P-v diagram for isothermal and isochoric processes.
- Students can compare final volumes for ideal-gas processes.
- Students can relate internal energy change to temperature change for an ideal gas.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- ideal gas
- same mass and same initial state for both systems

## Where students go wrong

- Reading the isochoric final volume as greater than the isothermal final volume.
- Using pressure change rather than temperature change to evaluate $\Delta U$ for an ideal gas.
- Missing that an isothermal ideal-gas process has $\Delta T = 0$ and therefore $\Delta U = 0$.
- Not using the same initial state and mass when comparing the two processes.

## How it is graded

Part a is worth +3. Parts b/c award +4 for noting $\Delta U_A = 0$ because $\Delta T=0$, and +3 for the comparison given in the solution.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Part a: process with higher volume at State 2: Process A ($V_{2A} > V_{2B}$)
- Part b: process with higher change in internal energy: Process B ($\Delta U_B > \Delta U_A$)
- Part c: explanation: For Process A, $\Delta T = 0$, so $\Delta U_A = 0$.
