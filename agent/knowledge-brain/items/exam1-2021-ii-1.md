## Problem

Part II, Short Problem 1 (20 points). A piston-cylinder device contains 2 kg of water at 420 K, where the fluid is initially a saturated liquid. Mechanical equilibrium is maintained as the piston slowly moves to State 2, where the quality of the water is 0.6. Identify any tables you use at each step of the problem.

a) (2 points) Write the assumptions necessary to solve for State 2 and the heat and work. *Enter these in the Canvas quiz — you do not need to also write them on the page.*

b) (+1) Calculate the change in volume in meters cubed [$\text{m}^3$]. *Enter this value in the Canvas quiz.*

c) (+1) Calculate the work in kilojoules [kJ]. *Enter this value in the Canvas quiz.*

d) (+1) Calculate the heat in kilojoules [kJ]. *Enter this value in the Canvas quiz.*

## What it tests

- Students can find saturated water properties from tables at a given temperature.
- Students can compute mixture specific volume and internal energy using quality.
- Students can evaluate boundary work for a quasi-equilibrium isobaric expansion.
- Students can apply the first law to find heat transfer.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Closed system (piston-cylinder)
- Mechanical equilibrium, so $P_1 = P_2$ and the expansion is isobaric
- Quasi-equilibrium process
- $\Delta KE = \Delta PE = 0$

## Where students go wrong

- Forgetting to use quality to find $v_2$ and $u_2$
- Using $v_f$ at State 2 instead of the two-phase mixture
- Using total volume $\mathcal{V}$ instead of specific volume in the work integral
- Sign error in work or heat
- Arithmetic error when computing heat from $\Delta U + W$

## How it is graded

Points reward stating assumptions, looking up saturated water properties at 420 K, computing mixture properties with quality, evaluating ${}_1W_2 = \int P\,d\mathcal{V}$ at $P_{sat}=0.437309$ MPa, and using the first law for heat. The boxed heat answer may not match the displayed $\Delta U + W$ arithmetic; reward the method.
