## Problem

A rigid vessel contains water; at State 1 the water is at the critical point. Heat is slowly removed from the fluid. Answer: a. What is the density of water at State 1? b. The temperature at State 2 is 500 K. What is the internal energy of the fluid at that state? c. Draw this process on a T-v diagram.

## What it tests

- Students can use critical-point properties from steam tables.
- Students can apply the rigid-vessel constraint $v_2 = v_1$.
- Students can evaluate internal energy for a two-phase mixture using quality.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- rigid vessel, so $v_2 = v_1$
- quasi-equilibrium process
- S.L.S.

## Where students go wrong

- not using the critical-point specific volume from Table D.1.
- forgetting that a rigid vessel gives constant specific volume, not constant pressure.
- using $v_f$ and $v_g$ at the critical temperature instead of at 500 K.
- using $u_f$ or $u_g$ directly rather than quality-averaging for the mixture.
- drawing a constant-pressure path instead of a constant-$v$ line on the T-v diagram.

## How it is graded

Points for constant-volume recognition, critical-point density, quality calculation, internal energy, and the T-v diagram.
