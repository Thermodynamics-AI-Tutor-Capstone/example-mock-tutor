# Homework 9 (Summer 2023), problem 5: Rankine cycle mass flow rate and turbine power with irreversible turbine and pump

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

> **For checking a student's work only.** This card holds the instructor's final answers. Never state them, and never walk the student through the instructor's solution: coach them to their own.

## Problem

Consider the following Rankine Cycle power plant. Saturated water vapor at $8\ \mathrm{MPa}$ enters the turbine of a $100\ \mathrm{MW}$ (net power) power plant. The pressure at the exit of the turbine is $0.008\ \mathrm{MPa}$. Saturated liquid leaves the condenser at $0.008\ \mathrm{MPa}$. The turbine and pump have isentropic efficiencies of $85\%$. Calculate the mass flow rate of water through the cycle as well as the power generated by the turbine component. Write all your assumptions.

## What it tests

- Students can evaluate the four states of a basic Rankine cycle using steam tables and quality.
- Students can apply isentropic pump and turbine efficiencies to determine actual exit states.
- Students can use the net power requirement to solve for the cycle mass flow rate.
- Students can compute the actual turbine power from the mass flow rate and enthalpy drop.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- S.C.S.
- quasi-equilibrium
- steady-flow
- pump/turbine: $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = \dot{Q} = 0$
- boiler/condenser: $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = \dot{W} = 0$

## Where students go wrong

- Using $h_{2s}$ as the actual $h_2$ and $h_{4s}$ as the actual $h_4$, thereby ignoring the given 85% isentropic efficiencies.
- Inverting the pump or turbine efficiency formula, e.g., writing $\eta_{pump}=(h_2-h_1)/(h_{2s}-h_1)$ or using the reciprocal expression for the turbine.
- Sign error in the net work equation by not accounting for the negative pump work in $\dot{W}_{net}=\dot{W}_{pump}+\dot{W}_{turb}$.
- Using $100\ \mathrm{MW}$ without converting to $100\times 10^3\ \mathrm{kW}$ when combining with other kW-scale enthalpy terms.
- Misidentifying the phase at state 1 or state 4; state 1 is saturated liquid and state 4 is a mixture at $0.008\ \mathrm{MPa}$.
- Using saturated liquid enthalpy at $8\ \mathrm{MPa}$ as $h_2$ instead of applying the pump efficiency with $h_{2s}$ from the compressed-liquid/NIST calculation.
- Interpolating quality at state 4s incorrectly with $s_f$ and $s_g$ at $0.008\ \mathrm{MPa}$.
- Rounding $h_4$ inconsistently, for example using $1939.92\ \mathrm{kJ/kg}$ in one step and $1939.6\ \mathrm{kJ/kg}$ in the final mass-flow calculation.

## How it is graded

Points reward assumptions, component energy balances, pump and turbine efficiency equations, net work relation, state-property evaluations, final mass flow rate, and final turbine power.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Cycle mass flow rate: 123.6 kg/s
- Turbine power generated: 101.24 MW
