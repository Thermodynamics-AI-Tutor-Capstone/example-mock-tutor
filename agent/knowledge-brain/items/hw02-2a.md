## Problem

Draw an isothermal process from $1\ \text{m}^3$ to $6\ \text{m}^3$ on a T-V diagram. Label your axes, the two states, and the path between them.

## What it tests

- Students can sketch an isothermal process on a T-V diagram and label axes, states, and path direction.

## Assumptions the solution expects



## Where students go wrong

- Drawing a curve instead of a horizontal line because isothermal on T-V means constant T.
- Placing state 1 at the larger volume and state 2 at the smaller volume.
- Forgetting the arrow or pointing it in the wrong direction.
- Not labeling axes or states.

## How it is graded

Credit for correctly labeled axes, two states, and the horizontal path with direction.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- T-V diagram: Horizontal line at constant T from state 1 at $V = 1\ \text{m}^3$ to state 2 at $V = 6\ \text{m}^3$, arrow pointing to the right; axes labeled $T$ (K) and $\mathcal{V}$ (m$^3$); dashed vertical line at $6\ \text{m}^3$.
