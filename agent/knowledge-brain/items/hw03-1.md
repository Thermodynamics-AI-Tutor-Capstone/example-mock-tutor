## Problem

1. (20 points) A sealed cylindrical vessel has the inner dimensions of 58.2 inches tall and 9 inches in diameter. The vessel holds 25 lb of nitrogen (N$_2$) at 2900 psi.
- Convert these specifications into SI units. What is the volume of the vessel in SI units?
- How many moles of nitrogen are in the vessel?
- What is the particular gas constant of nitrogen?
- Assuming nitrogen is an ideal gas, what is the temperature of the nitrogen in the vessel?
- Now I replace the nitrogen with the same number of moles of Argon at the same pressure. What is the temperature in the vessel now?

## What it tests

- Convert length, diameter, mass, and pressure to SI units
- Calculate vessel volume from cylinder geometry
- Calculate moles from mass and molecular weight
- Compute particular gas constant from universal gas constant
- Apply the ideal-gas law to find temperature
- Recognize that replacing the gas with the same $P,V,N,\bar{R}$ gives the same temperature

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Ideal gas for nitrogen and argon
- Vessel is sealed and rigid, so $V$ and $N$ stay fixed in the replacement step

## Where students go wrong

- Converting only some quantities to SI before applying $PV=MRT$
- Using atomic nitrogen (14.0067 g/mol) instead of N$_2$ (28.0134 g/mol)
- Using the universal gas constant in the mass form or the particular gas constant in the mole form
- Forgetting that volume must be in m$^3$ and pressure in Pa when calculating temperature
- For Argon, expecting the temperature to change because the particular gas constant changes, rather than recognizing that $P,V,N,\bar{R}$ are the same

## How it is graded

The solution awards points for unit conversions (+1/2 each), volume (+2), moles (+2+1), particular R (+2+1+1), ideal-gas temperature (+2+1), and argon temperature (+2+1). Transcript inconsistency: the written volume from cylinder geometry is 0.09 m$^3$, but the temperature calculation uses 0.019 m$^3$; flag this rather than treating it as a correct volume.
