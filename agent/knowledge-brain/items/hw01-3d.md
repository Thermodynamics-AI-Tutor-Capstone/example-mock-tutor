# Homework 1 (Summer 2023), problem 3d: Natural gas recompression station energy conservation

## Problem

Consider these five conservation scenarios and provide: indicate whether it is appropriate to use a finite-time or instantaneous conservation formulation; write the form of the conservation equation, cancelling any terms not required and explaining why they can be neglected. d. Natural gas is used in a wide range of applications, from power generation to home heating. In order to move natural gas around the country, there is a network of gas pipelines where gas flows from wells to local distribution networks. In order to keep the gas moving, you need to "recompress" the gas along the pipeline in a gas recompression station. Here, gas flows through at a rate of $\dot{m}_{gas}$ and a compressor does work on the gas to raise its pressure at a rate of $\dot{W}_{compressor}$. Write a conservation of energy statement for the gas in this scenario.

## What it tests

- Students can choose finite-time vs instantaneous conservation formulations based on flow rates.
- Students can simplify an energy conservation equation for a flow device with work input.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Instantaneous formulation because the scenario gives flow and work rates.
- Mass crosses the control volume.
- No energy generation.

## Where students go wrong

- Using finite-time even though the scenario gives rates.
- Omitting mass flow terms or the compressor work term.
- Including an energy generation term.
- Adding mass flow rates directly to a work rate as if they were the same type of term, which mixes mass and energy rate units.

## How it is graded

+2 for instantaneous; +2 for generic conservation equation; +2 for final simplified equation.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- formulation: instantaneous
- conservation of energy equation: \frac{dE_{pipe}}{dt} = \dot{m}_{in} + \dot{W}_{comp} - \dot{m}_{out}
