## Problem

Steam expands isentropically in a piston-cylinder arrangement from a pressure of $P_1 = 2 \ \mathrm{MPa}$ and a temperature of $T_1 = 500 \ \mathrm{K}$ to a saturated vapor at State 2. (a) Draw this process on a T-s diagram. (b) Calculate the mass-specific entropy at State 1. (c) What is the mass-specific entropy at State 2? (d) Calculate the pressure and temperature at State 2.

## What it tests

- Students can locate superheated and saturated-vapor states on a T-s diagram.
- Students can find superheated-steam entropy using property data.
- Students can apply $s_2 = s_1$ for an isentropic process.
- Students can interpolate saturation pressure and temperature from entropy.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- simple compressible substance
- quasi-equilibrium
- isentropic
- State 1 is vapor because $T_1 > T_{sat}$ at 2 MPa

## Where students go wrong

- Drawing the isentropic process as non-vertical on the T-s diagram.
- Not recognizing State 1 is superheated vapor at 2 MPa and 500 K.
- Not setting $s_2 = s_1$; instead using $s_2$ as saturated-vapor entropy at the original pressure.
- Using ideal-gas entropy relations for steam instead of property tables.
- Interpolating incorrectly between the saturated-vapor entropy values at 1.5 MPa and 1.6 MPa.

## How it is graded

Points for diagram, assumptions, using property data for s1, s2=s1, and interpolation for T2 and P2.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $s_1$: 6.4265 kJ/(kg-K)
- $s_2$: 6.4265 kJ/(kg-K)
- $T_2$: 473.64 K
- $P_2$: 1.57 MPa
