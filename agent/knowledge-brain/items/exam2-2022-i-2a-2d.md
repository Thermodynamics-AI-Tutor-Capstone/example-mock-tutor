## Problem

Identify the correct sign of the change in entropy for the following three processes.

a) An ideal gas undergoes an isothermal increase in pressure.

b) An ideal gas undergoes isochoric temperature increase.

c) An ideal gas undergoes an adiabatic and irreversible expansion.

d) Identify the equations you used to explain the answers to the previous three questions.

## What it tests

- Students can determine the sign of entropy change using ideal-gas entropy relations.
- Students can use the entropy balance with generation for an irreversible adiabatic process.

## Assumptions the solution expects



## Where students go wrong

- Thinking an isothermal process always has zero entropy change even though pressure changes.
- Using c_p instead of c_v for the isochoric process, or ignoring the temperature term entirely.
- Assuming adiabatic implies isentropic and ignoring the entropy generation term (misc:m15-adiabatic-implies-isentropic).
- Writing the ideal-gas entropy relation with the wrong sign on the pressure-ratio or volume-ratio term.

## How it is graded

2 points each for the three multiple-choice signs; 2 points for each of the three equations/explanations in part d. The solution uses the ideal-gas T-P and T-v entropy relations and the entropy balance with Phi.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Change in entropy for isothermal pressure increase: LESS THAN ZERO
- Change in entropy for isochoric temperature increase: GREATER THAN ZERO
- Change in entropy for adiabatic irreversible expansion: GREATER THAN ZERO
- Equations used: \Delta s = c_p \ln(T_2/T_1) - R \ln(P_2/P_1); \Delta s = c_v \ln(T_2/T_1) + R \ln(v_2/v_1); \Delta S = \int \delta Q/T + \Phi
