## Problem

Can the thermal efficiency of a heat engine ever reach one? Answer the multiple-choice question YES or NO, then explain your response.

## What it tests

- Students can apply the Kelvin-Planck statement of the second law to bound heat-engine thermal efficiency.

## Assumptions the solution expects



## Where students go wrong

- Saying YES because energy is conserved, without recognizing that heat cannot be fully converted to work in a cycle.
- Confusing thermal efficiency with COP or using the Carnot efficiency expression incorrectly for a general heat engine.
- Restating the first law as the reason instead of the second law / Kelvin-Planck statement.

## How it is graded

2 points for selecting NO; 3 points for explaining that Kelvin-Planck forbids complete heat-to-work conversion, so eta_th = Wdot_net/Qdot_in < 1.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Correct choice: NO
