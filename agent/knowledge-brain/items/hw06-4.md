# Homework 6 (Summer 2023), problem 4: Two-fluid heat exchanger with air and water

## Problem

In an experiment in my lab we use hot air to vaporize water before putting it into the combustor (this is real and we really did this calculation earlier this year!). Treat this problem like a two-fluid heat exchanger, where the air ($c_p = 1001$ J/kg-K) enters the heat exchanger at 623 K and the water enters the heat exchanger as a saturated liquid at 0.1 MPa. Use an air flow rate of 0.05 kg/s and the water flow rate is 0.002 kg/s. What is the temperature of the air leaving the heat exchanger if the water leaves as a saturated vapor? How much heat was transferred? Make sure to list all assumptions you used.

## What it tests

- Students can apply the steady-flow energy equation separately to each fluid in a two-fluid heat exchanger.
- Students can use steam tables to find water enthalpy at saturated liquid and saturated vapor states.
- Students can relate the heat lost by air to the heat gained by water and compute the air outlet temperature.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- air is an ideal gas
- $c_p$ for air is constant
- S.C.S.
- quasi-equilibrium
- steady flow
- $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = 0$
- $\dot{W} = 0$
- no heat loss to the outside
- water side is isobaric at 0.1 MPa

## Where students go wrong

- Using $Q = mc_p\Delta T$ for the water side instead of steam-table enthalpy changes.
- Forgetting the sign convention: the air loses heat, so $\dot{Q}_{air} = -\dot{Q}_{H_2O}$.
- Adding the heat transfer to the air inlet temperature with the wrong sign, which would make the air outlet hotter than the inlet.
- Mixing J/kg-K, kg/s, and kJ/kg without converting so the final answer is in kW.
- Using saturated liquid or vapor property values at the wrong pressure.
- Not stating heat-exchanger assumptions such as no external heat loss, $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = 0$, and $\dot{W} = 0$.

## How it is graded

Credit is given for assumptions, applying the steady-flow energy equation to the water and air streams, table lookups for saturated water at 0.1 MPa, computing the water-side heat transfer, correctly assigning the negative air-side heat transfer, and computing the air outlet temperature.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $\dot{Q}_{H_2O}$: 4.51 kW
- $T_{air,2}$: 532.9 K
