## Problem

Part I, Question 2 (10 points). There are two piston-cylinder devices — one filled with 1 kg of water and one with 1 kg of air. Both undergo isothermal processes at 450 K from 0.002 m$^3$ to 0.2 m$^3$. Which one has a larger change in internal energy? Explain what's happening in both cylinders. *Type your answer in the box in Canvas.*

## What it tests

- Students can distinguish ideal-gas internal-energy behavior from phase-change water behavior.
- Students can use saturation tables to identify a two-phase mixture.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Air is treated as an ideal gas, so $u = u(T)$ and $\Delta U = 0$ for the isothermal air process
- Water properties are read from Table D1 at 450 K: $v_f = 0.0011232$ m$^3$/kg and $v_g = 0.20781$ m$^3$/kg
- Water remains a saturated mixture over the range, with quality increasing as vapor is formed

## Where students go wrong

- Assuming water also has zero internal-energy change because the process is isothermal
- Treating water as an ideal gas
- Not checking the water saturation table
- Confusing specific volume with total volume

## How it is graded

Solution rewards ideal-gas $u(T)$ statement, air $\Delta U=0$, Table D1 water values, and water larger than air.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Larger change in internal energy: Water
- $\Delta U$ of air$: 0 kJ
- $\Delta U$ of water$: >0 kJ
