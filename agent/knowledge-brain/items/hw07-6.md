# Homework 7 (Summer 2023), problem 6: Thermocline heat engine maximum efficiency

## Problem

Thermoclines are temperature gradients in bodies of water; freshwater lake temperatures can jump up to $30^\circ$F in a few feet. It is proposed to take advantage of this temperature difference and construct a small power plant that absorbs heat from warm water near the surface and rejects waste heat to cold water a few meters below in one of the Great Lakes. (p. 2)

- a. Determine the maximum thermal efficiency if the water temperatures at the two respective locations are $24$ C and $3$ C.
- b. Draw a diagram of this process showing where heat and work transfer take place.
- c. Identify the reservoirs used in this heat engine and why they're reservoirs.
- d. What potential irreversibilities may arise in building this power plant? (p. 2)

## What it tests

- Students can convert Celsius temperatures to Kelvin for thermodynamic calculations.
- Students can calculate the Carnot efficiency for two thermal reservoirs.
- Students can sketch a heat engine identifying heat and work transfers.
- Students can justify why large bodies of water are thermal reservoirs.
- Students can name plausible irreversibilities in a real power plant.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- quasi-static/steady-state operation for maximum efficiency
- maximum thermal efficiency means no losses/irreversibilities
- surface water and deep water act as thermal reservoirs

## Where students go wrong

- Using Celsius temperatures directly in $1-T_L/T_H$ instead of converting to Kelvin.
- Computing the maximum efficiency with finite-temperature heat exchange or friction included.
- Drawing heat/work arrows in the wrong direction.
- Claiming the lake is a reservoir only because it is large, without explaining negligible temperature change.
- Listing only friction and missing finite-temperature heat transfer or turbulent mixing.

## How it is graded

Part a: +1 each for converting both Celsius temperatures to Kelvin, +1 for $\eta_{th,max}=1-T_L/T_H$ and result. Part b: +1 each for high-$T$ reservoir, $\dot{Q}_H$ arrow, engine, $\dot{W}_{net}$ arrow, $\dot{Q}_L$ arrow, low-$T$ reservoir, thermal reservoir marking. Part c: +2 for lake-reservoir explanation. Part d: +2 for any plausible irreversibility; acceptable examples include friction, finite-ΔT heat transfer, turbulent mixing.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $T_H$: 297.15 K
- $T_L$: 276.15 K
- $\eta_{th,max}$: 7.07 %
- Part b diagram: High-temperature reservoir at $T_H$ supplies $\dot{Q}_H$ to engine; engine delivers $\dot{W}_{net}$; engine rejects $\dot{Q}_L$ to low-temperature reservoir at $T_L$; both reservoirs labeled thermal.
- Part c reservoirs: Warm surface water is the high-temperature reservoir and cold deep water is the low-temperature reservoir. A Great Lake is a reservoir because engine operation will not change the temperature of the entire lake.
- Part d irreversibilities: Friction; heat exchange across a finite temperature difference; turbulent mixing.
