# Homework 8 (Summer 2023), problem 3: Mass-specific entropy changes for ideal gas and water

## Problem

Calculate the change in mass-specific entropy in the following situations. Identify which assumptions you use for each of the calculations. Use the following properties for air: $R = 287 \ \mathrm{J/(kg \cdot K)}$ and $c_v = 720 \ \mathrm{J/(kg \cdot K)}$. (a) Isothermal compression of air from $10 \ \mathrm{m^3/kg}$ to $4 \ \mathrm{m^3/kg}$. (b) Isothermal compression of air from $0.1 \ \mathrm{MPa}$ to $1.7 \ \mathrm{MPa}$. (c) Isobaric heating of air from $300 \ \mathrm{K}$ to $1200 \ \mathrm{K}$. (d) Isobaric heating of water at $1 \ \mathrm{MPa}$ from a saturated liquid to a saturated vapor.

## What it tests

- Students can select appropriate ideal-gas entropy relations for isothermal and isobaric processes.
- Students can identify ideal-gas, constant-specific-heats, and quasi-equilibrium assumptions.
- Students can compute $c_p = c_v + R$ for air.
- Students can use saturation tables to compute entropy change for water vaporization.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- air is ideal gas
- constant specific heats
- quasi-equilibrium
- $c_p = c_v + R = 1007$ J/(kg-K)
- water entropy change from saturated liquid/vapor values at 1 MPa

## Where students go wrong

- Using $c_p$ instead of $c_v$ in the T-v ideal-gas entropy relation or vice versa.
- Not setting the isothermal term to zero in parts (a) and (b), or the isobaric term to zero in part (c).
- Forgetting to compute $c_p = c_v + R$ and using $c_v = 720$ J/(kg-K) for isobaric heating.
- Misplacing the negative sign for compression processes.
- Treating water as an ideal gas instead of using saturation property values at 1 MPa.
- Mixing kJ and J units between gas and water parts.

## How it is graded

Points for stating assumptions, using correct entropy relation, computing c_p, and reading saturated-water entropies.
