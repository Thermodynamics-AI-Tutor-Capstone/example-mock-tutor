# Exam 2 (Summer 2023), problem II.2: Reversible cycle with air: efficiency, work, and process states

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

## Problem

A reversible cycle operates between a maximum temperature of 1800 K and a minimum temperature of 300 K with a working fluid of air (\(R = 287\) J/kg-K, \(c_p = 1001\) J/kg-K, and \(\gamma = 1.4\)). Answer the following questions about the cycle and the processes in the cycle.

a) (3 points) What is the thermal efficiency of this cycle?

b) (6 points) If 100 kJ of heat is added to the cycle, how much net work is produced by the cycle in [kJ]?

c) (2 points) What are the assumptions necessary to solve for any of the states in the cycle or the performance of any of its components?

d) (7 points) The first step of the cycle is an adiabatic compression from \(T_1 = 300\) K and \(P_1 = 0.1\) MPa to a pressure of \(P_2 = 1.2\) MPa. What is the temperature of the air after that first process in [K]?

e) (7 points) The second step of the cycle is an isobaric heat addition step in a passive heat exchanger that brings the fluid to its maximum temperature in the cycle. If the mass flow rate of air is 10 kg/s, what is the rate of heat addition in this step in [MW]?

## What it tests

- Students can compute the thermal efficiency of a reversible cycle using reservoir temperatures.
- Students can relate net work to heat input through thermal efficiency.
- Students can apply the isentropic ideal-gas relation \(T_2=T_1(P_2/P_1)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}\).
- Students can apply the steady-flow energy equation to an isobaric heat exchanger.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Reversible cycle.
- Ideal gas with constant specific heats.
- Steady flow.
- For part (d): adiabatic reversible compression, so isentropic.
- For part (e): steady flow, negligible kinetic and potential energy changes, no work in the passive heat exchanger.

## Where students go wrong

- Reversing the Carnot temperature ratio, e.g. using \(1 - T_H/T_L\), giving an impossible or negative efficiency.
- Using temperatures in Celsius or forgetting to use absolute temperatures.
- Forgetting \(\eta_{th}=W_{net}/Q_{in}\) and treating the 100 kJ heat addition as the net work.
- Using the wrong isentropic exponent, e.g. \(T_2=T_1(P_2/P_1)^\gamma\) or \(T_2=T_1(P_2/P_1)^{\gamma/(\gamma-1)}\).
- Treating the adiabatic reversible compression as non-isentropic and looking up property tables instead of using the ideal-gas isentropic relation.
- Forgetting to convert \(c_p=1001\) J/kg-K to kJ/kg-K when computing heat rate in MW.
- Including shaft work or kinetic/potential energy terms in the heat exchanger when they should be zero.

## How it is graded

Part (a): +2 for Carnot efficiency formula, +2 for answer. Part (b): +2 for \(\eta=W_{net}/Q_{in}\), +2 for answer. Part (c): four half-point assumptions. Part (d): +3 for isentropic relation, +2 for substitution, +2 for answer. Part (e): +2 for first law, +3 for \(\dot{Q}=\dot{m}c_p(T_3-T_2)\), +2 for answer.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Thermal efficiency: 0.83
- Net work produced: 83 kJ
- Temperature after adiabatic compression: 610.18 K
- Rate of heat addition in isobaric step: 11.91 MW
