## Problem

Consider the five scenarios from the previous question and answer: Indicate whether we should use a system or control volume analysis and why. Draw the scenario and then draw the system or control volume. Label the system/control volume, boundary/control surface, and surroundings. Label the energy and/or mass flows in and/or out of the system/control volume. e. Battery scenario from Question 3: $E_{solar}$ Joules of energy would be captured by the solar panels each day, $Q_{loss}$ Joules would be lost from the system through electrical inefficiencies, and $E_{house}$ Joules of energy would be used by the house during the day.

## What it tests

- Students can distinguish system vs control volume based on whether the battery has constant mass.
- Students can label boundaries, surroundings, and energy flows.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- System because the battery is constant mass.
- Drawing must label system, boundary, surroundings, energy input, energy output, and heat loss.

## Where students go wrong

- Choosing a control volume even though battery mass is fixed.
- Not labeling the energy input, energy output, or heat loss.
- Not labeling the boundary or surroundings.

## How it is graded

+1 for system; +1 for reason; +4 for labeled drawing.

- analysis type: system
- reason: battery is constant mass
