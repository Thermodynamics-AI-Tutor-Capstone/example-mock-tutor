# Exam 1 (Summer 2023), problem I.3: Adiabatic expansion in a piston-cylinder

## Problem

A fluid in a piston-cylinder system expands adiabatically from volume $V_1$ to $V_2$, where $V_2>V_1$.

a) What is the sign of work for this process?
b) Is the work done on the fluid or by the fluid?
c) What is the sign of the change in internal energy of the fluid in this process?
d) Explain your answer to part c using an equation.

## What it tests

- Students can determine the sign of boundary work from expansion or compression.
- Students can identify whether work is done by or on a system.
- Students can apply the first law to an adiabatic process.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- adiabatic expansion
- closed piston-cylinder system

## Where students go wrong

- Choosing negative work for an expansion.
- Saying the work is done on the fluid instead of by the fluid.
- Forgetting that $Q=0$ for an adiabatic process.
- Using an incorrect sign convention in the first law.
- Concluding $\Delta U$ is positive without applying the first law.

## How it is graded

Part a +2 for work sign; part b +2 for work by the fluid; parts c/d +3 for the first-law equation with adiabatic $Q=0$ and +2 for $\Delta U < 0$.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Part a: sign of work: positive
- Part b: work done by or on the fluid: by the fluid
- $Part c: sign of $\Delta U$: negative
- Part d: explanation: ${}_1W_2 = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} P\,dV > 0$ because $V_2 > V_1$; with ${}_1Q_2 = 0$, $\Delta U = {}_1Q_2 - {}_1W_2 = -{}_1W_2 < 0$.
