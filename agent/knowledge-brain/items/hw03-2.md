## Problem

2. (10 points) A pressure transducer is hooked up to the vessel with N$_2$ (not N) in Problem #1, and the transducer reads gage pressure. Assume that the pressure from Problem #1 is the absolute pressure.
- What is the gage pressure read by the transducer in SI units if the vessel is located in Washington DC (sea level)?
- What happens to the gage pressure if I now take the vessel to Denver, CO?

## What it tests

- Relate absolute pressure and gage pressure
- Use standard sea-level atmospheric pressure
- Use a different atmospheric pressure for Denver
- Predict the change in gage pressure when atmospheric pressure changes

## Assumptions the solution expects

- $P$ from Problem 1 is absolute pressure
- Washington DC is at sea level with $P_{atm}=101325$ Pa
- Denver atmospheric pressure is $71705.48$ Pa
- $P_{abs}=P_{gage}+P_{atm}$

## Where students go wrong

- Using $P_{gage}=P_{abs}+P_{atm}$ instead of $P_{gage}=P_{abs}-P_{atm}$
- Not converting the atmospheric pressure to Pa
- Using the same sea-level atmospheric pressure for Denver
- Assuming the gage pressure is unchanged when moving to Denver

## How it is graded

Part (a) awards points for the absolute/gage relation (+3), the subtraction (+2), and answer (+2). Part (b) awards points for using the Denver atmospheric pressure (+2) and answer (+1).

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Gage pressure at Washington DC: 19888675 Pa
- Gage pressure at Denver: 19918294.52 Pa
- Change in gage pressure: increases
