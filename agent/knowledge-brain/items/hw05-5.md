## Problem

5. (20 points) Consider a piston-cylinder system with 1 kg of water that undergoes the cycle in the table below. Processes 1-2 and 3-4 are adiabatic. Calculate the work and heat transfer for each of the processes, and the net work and net heat transfer of the entire cycle. Feel free to use the NIST webbook to calculate properties you need if they are not present on the table. Discuss the result of the net work and net heat transfer - these numbers should look interesting to you when you calculate them.

| State | P [MPa] | T [K] | v [m$^3$/kg] |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.6 | 499.2 | 0.0012014 |
| 2 | 5 | 500 | 0.0012 |
| 3 | 5 | 578.5 | 0.046114 |
| 4 | 2.6 | 499.2 | 0.076899 |

## What it tests

- Students can identify the phase of water from pressure, temperature, and specific volume data
- Students can evaluate boundary work for constant-pressure segments using specific volumes
- Students can apply the first law to adiabatic and non-adiabatic processes
- Students can compute net work and net heat transfer for a closed-system cycle and check consistency

## Assumptions the solution expects

- S.C.S.
- Negligible kinetic and potential energy changes, $\Delta PE=\Delta KE=0$
- Quasi-equilibrium
- ${}_1Q_2=0$ and ${}_3Q_4=0$ because processes 1-2 and 3-4 are adiabatic
- Processes 2-3 and 4-1 are constant-pressure segments, as read from equal pressures in the state table

## Where students go wrong

- Misidentifying state 2 as a saturated mixture rather than a liquid because $T<T_{sat}$ at 5 MPa
- Misidentifying state 1 or state 4; state 1 is saturated liquid at 2.6 MPa and state 4 is saturated vapor at 2.6 MPa per the solution
- Using the wrong internal energy value for the phase or pressure at a state
- Forgetting to multiply specific-volume differences by the 1 kg mass when computing boundary work
- Sign error in adiabatic processes: using ${}_1W_2=+\Delta U$ instead of ${}_1W_2=-\Delta U$
- Sign error in process 4-1 work by using $v_4-v_1$ instead of $v_1-v_4$
- Failing to check that $W_{net}=Q_{net}$ and that the net internal energy change over the cycle is zero

## How it is graded

Points reward identifying phase and internal energy at each state, applying the first law with adiabatic processes, computing constant-pressure boundary work, and checking that net work equals net heat transfer with zero net internal-energy change over the cycle.
