# Homework 8 (Summer 2023), problem 6: Isentropic diesel-engine compression plots and emissions

## Problem

Before starting at Penn State, I worked at Sandia National Laboratories in an engine lab where we studied combustion processes in diesel engines. One thing we could not do was measure the temperature at the top of the compression stroke. Our engine had a compression ratio of $\mathcal{V}_1/\mathcal{V}_2 = 11.2$. Because of the low compression ratio, we had to boost the incoming air to get the right thermodynamic conditions at the top of the compression stroke. In this problem, calculate the temperature and pressure at the top of an isentropic compression in this engine for a range of operating conditions; assume the ratio of specific heats of air is 1.4. (a) Use Excel or Matlab to plot pressure after an isentropic compression for $T_1 = 320 \ \mathrm{K}$ as a function of initial pressure for $P_1 = 0.1 \ \mathrm{MPa}$ to $0.2 \ \mathrm{MPa}$. (b) Use Excel or Matlab to plot temperature after an isentropic compression for $P_1 = 0.15 \ \mathrm{MPa}$ and a range of temperatures from $T_1 = 280 \ \mathrm{K}$ to $350 \ \mathrm{K}$. (c) Read about the work at https://www.energy.gov/eere/vehicles/advanced-combustion-strategies and discuss at least one strategy for reducing engine emissions in diesel engines. How could the thermodynamic condition at the top of a compression stroke, right before combustion, change emissions?

## What it tests

- Students can model the compression stroke as isentropic using ideal-gas relations.
- Students can compute and plot final pressure and temperature over a range of initial conditions.
- Students can connect top-of-compression thermodynamic conditions to diesel emissions qualitatively.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- ideal gas
- constant c_p and c_v
- isentropic
- quasi-equilibrium

## Where students go wrong

- Using the wrong exponent in the isentropic compression relations, such as $\gamma$ for temperature and $\gamma-1$ for pressure.
- Using the reciprocal of the compression ratio or not using $\mathcal{V}_1/\mathcal{V}_2 = 11.2$.
- For part (b), not holding $P_1 = 0.15$ MPa fixed while varying $T_1$.
- For part (c), giving an emissions strategy without linking it to the temperature/pressure at top of compression.

## How it is graded

Points for assumptions, using correct compression relations, and correct plots; part (c) awarded no points (any reasonable answer accepted).

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Pressure after compression (a): P_2 = P_1 (\mathcal{V}_1/\mathcal{V}_2)^\gamma
- Temperature after compression (b): T_2 = T_1 (\mathcal{V}_1/\mathcal{V}_2)^{\gamma-1}
