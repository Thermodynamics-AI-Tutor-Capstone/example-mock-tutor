# Homework 7 (Summer 2023), problem 3: Steam turbine power output

## Problem

A steam turbine operating at a flow rate of $\dot{m}=10$ kg/s expands steam from $P_1=16$ MPa and $T_1=900$ K to saturated vapor at $P_2=0.1$ MPa. How much power does the turbine produce? List all assumptions and tables used. (p. 2) Note: the instructor solutions transcript states $P_1=10$ MPa and uses that value in the calculation.

## What it tests

- Students can evaluate turbine power using the steady-flow energy equation.
- Students can determine enthalpy from superheated steam tables given pressure and temperature.
- Students can determine enthalpy of saturated vapor from pressure tables.
- Students can state appropriate turbine assumptions.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- quasi-equilibrium
- steady flow
- turbine is adiabatic: $\dot{Q}=0$
- negligible kinetic energy change: $\Delta ke=0$
- negligible potential energy change: $\Delta pe=0$

## Where students go wrong

- Using $P_1=16$ MPa from the problem sheet rather than the $10$ MPa used in the solutions transcript, changing the superheated-table lookup.
- Not recognizing state 2 as saturated vapor; using compressed-liquid or superheated table instead of $h_g$ at $P_2=0.1$ MPa.
- Incorrect sign on turbine work in $\dot{m}\Delta h=-\dot{W}$.
- Forgetting to convert kJ/kg and kg/s to MW, or dropping a factor of $10^3$.
- Reading $h_f$ instead of $h_g$ for saturated vapor.
- Not listing assumptions or tables even though the problem asks for them.

## How it is graded

Assumptions +1 each for quasi-equilibrium, steady flow, and adiabatic/no-ke/no-pe turbine; +2 general steady-flow energy equation; +2 turbine reduced equation; state 1 recognition +1 and $h_1$ +2; state 2 recognition +1 and $h_2$ +2; final answer +2; correct units +1. Solutions transcript uses $P_1=10$ MPa, not 16 MPa.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $h_1$ at $P_1=10$ MPa, $T_1=900$ K$: 3691.6 kJ/kg
- $h_2=h_g$ at $P_2=0.1$ MPa$: 2674.9 kJ/kg
- $\dot{W}$: 10.167 MW
