# Homework 1 (Summer 2023), problem 3e: Battery energy conservation over a day

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

## Problem

Consider these five conservation scenarios and provide: indicate whether it is appropriate to use a finite-time or instantaneous conservation formulation; write the form of the conservation equation, cancelling any terms not required and explaining why they can be neglected. e. Penn State has a solar farm on campus that can produce up to 2 MW of power. Although our solar farm doesn't have one, many new solar installations have battery packs to store the energy. In this system, $E_{solar}$ Joules of energy would be captured by the solar panels each day and $Q_{loss}$ Joules of energy would be lost from the system through electrical inefficiencies during the same time period. During the day, the power goes out and the house uses $E_{house}$ Joules of energy. Write a conservation of energy statement for the energy in the battery during the day.

## What it tests

- Students can choose finite-time vs instantaneous conservation formulations based on a finite duration.
- Students can simplify an energy conservation equation for a storage system with losses and output.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Finite-time formulation because the process takes place over the course of a day.
- No energy generation.

## Where students go wrong

- Using instantaneous even though the scenario is framed over a day.
- Forgetting the heat loss term or the energy used by the house.
- Including an energy generation term.

## How it is graded

+2 for finite time; +2 for generic conservation equation; +2 for final simplified equation.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- formulation: finite time
- conservation of energy equation: \Delta E_{\text{balancing}} = E_{\text{stored}} - Q_{\text{loss}} - E_{out}
