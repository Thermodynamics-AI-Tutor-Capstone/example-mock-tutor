# Homework 6 (Summer 2023), problem 3: Throttle with saturated-vapor water

## Problem

A throttle is used to reduce the pressure of steady-flowing water in a processing plant. The water enters the throttle at a pressure of 5 MPa as a saturated vapor and leaves the throttle at a pressure of 3 MPa. What is the temperature and mass-specific internal energy of the water as it leaves the throttle?

## What it tests

- Students can model a throttle as an isenthalpic steady-flow device.
- Students can use steam tables to identify the exit state as a saturated mixture.
- Students can compute quality and mass-specific internal energy for a saturated mixture.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- S.C.S.
- quasi-equilibrium
- steady flow
- $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = 0$
- $\dot{Q} = 0$
- $\dot{W} = 0$
- throttle is isenthalpic, so $h_2 = h_1$

## Where students go wrong

- Treating water as an ideal gas or using $c_p\Delta T$ instead of steam tables.
- Assuming the exit is saturated vapor just because the inlet is saturated vapor; at 3 MPa the enthalpy is between $h_f$ and $h_g$, so the exit is a saturated mixture.
- Reading the saturation temperature at the wrong pressure from the steam tables.
- Computing $u_2 = h_2$ or using only $u_g$; a throttled two-phase state requires the quality-weighted average.
- Mixing kJ/kg and J/kg units in the property calculations.

## How it is graded

Credit is given for assumptions, the steady-flow energy balance reducing to $\Delta h = 0$, obtaining $h_1$ from saturated-vapor data at 5 MPa, recognizing the 3 MPa exit state as a mixture, finding $T_2$ and $x_2$, and computing $u_2$ from quality.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $T_2$: 507 K
- $u_2$: 2595.2 kJ/kg
