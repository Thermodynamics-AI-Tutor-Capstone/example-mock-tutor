# Homework 9 (Summer 2023), problem 1: Steam turbine actual work, entropy change, ideal work, and feasibility

## Problem

Consider the following steam turbine with a mass flow rate of $15\ \mathrm{kg/s}$. (a) Measurements from the power plant of the actual (non-ideal) turbine show that the inlet pressure is $P_1=12\ \mathrm{MPa}$ and inlet temperature is $T_1=640\ \mathrm{K}$. The outlet pressure is $P_2=1\ \mathrm{MPa}$ and a quality of $x_2=0.86$. Calculate the work output of this machine and the change in the entropy of the fluid across the turbine. (b) Recalculate the work for an ideal turbine assuming the same initial state and final pressure, and use that to calculate the component efficiency. (c) Would this process be possible if the final quality is $0.8$? Why or why not?

## What it tests

- Students can calculate actual and isentropic turbine work from steam-table properties and quality.
- Students can compute entropy change across a steady-flow turbine.
- Students can evaluate whether a proposed turbine exit state is possible by comparing exit and inlet specific entropy.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- S.L.S.
- Steady-flow
- quasi-equilibrium
- $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = \dot{Q} = 0$

## Where students go wrong

- Using $s_2=s_1$ for the actual turbine entropy change; in part (a), $s_2$ must come from $x_2$ and the saturated mixture values.
- Using the actual $h_2$ in the ideal turbine calculation instead of computing $h_{2s}$ from $s_{2s}=s_1$.
- Inverting turbine efficiency, for example using $\dot{W}_{ideal}/\dot{W}_{real}$ instead of $\dot{W}_{real}/\dot{W}_{ideal}$.
- Sign error in turbine work: writing $\dot{W}=\dot{m}(h_2-h_1)$ without the required negative sign.
- In part (c), trying to decide feasibility by comparing quality or enthalpy rather than checking $s_2 \ge s_1$.
- Miscalculating $s_2$ at $x_2=0.8$ from the saturated liquid and vapor entropy values.
- Forgetting the $(1-x)s_f$ term when computing mixture entropy and enthalpy.

## How it is graded

Points reward assumptions, the steady-flow energy equation, correct steam-table property lookup, actual and ideal work calculations, efficiency calculation, and the second-law feasibility check. Specific point markings are shown in the solution pages.
