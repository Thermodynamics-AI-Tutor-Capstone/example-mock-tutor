# Homework 7 (Summer 2023), problem 2: High-bypass turbofan fan and bypass nozzle analysis

## Problem

In commercial aviation, jet engines are high-bypass engines: the air flow through the fan is much larger than through the compressor, typically 8–12 times the core flow, and most thrust comes from the bypass flow. Analyze the bypass system, composed of the fan and bypass nozzle. Working fluid is air with constant $c_p = 1001$ J/kg-K; the fan works like a compressor. (p. 1)

- a. For bypass mass flow $\dot{m}=50$ kg/s, inlet temperature $T_1=300$ K, inlet velocity $V_1=170$ m/s, and fan work input $\dot{W}=-2.7457$ MW, find the air temperature after the fan. (p. 2)

- b. The air then flows into the nozzle. At nozzle inlet, the problem sheet gives density $\rho_2=1.7674$ kg/m$^3$ (the solutions transcript writes $1.7474$ kg/m$^3$); area ratio $A_{inlet}/A_{outlet}=3$. If velocity at the nozzle outlet is $V_3=300$ m/s, find the outlet density and temperature. (p. 2)

## What it tests

- Students can apply the steady-flow energy equation to a fan modeled as a compressor.
- Students can compute temperature rise from constant-$c_p$ ideal gas and fan work.
- Students can apply mass conservation to a nozzle to find exit density.
- Students can apply the nozzle energy equation to find exit temperature.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- air is an ideal gas
- steady flow
- constant $c_p$
- fan: $\dot{Q}=\Delta ke=\Delta pe=0$
- nozzle: $\dot{W}=\dot{Q}=\Delta pe=0$
- fan kinetic energy change is zero, so nozzle inlet velocity equals fan inlet velocity: $V_2=V_1=170$ m/s

## Where students go wrong

- Incorrect sign on fan work in $\dot{m}c_p(T_2-T_1)=-\dot{W}$; writing $=\dot{W}$ yields the wrong temperature.
- Not converting $-2.7457$ MW to watts when using $c_p$ in J/kg-K.
- Using a nozzle inlet velocity other than $170$ m/s without recognizing the fan assumption $\Delta ke=0$.
- Inverting the area ratio or velocity ratio in $\rho_3=\rho_2 V_2 A_2/(V_3 A_3)$.
- Using the solutions transcript's $1.7474$ kg/m$^3$ instead of the problem sheet's $1.7674$ kg/m$^3$.
- Forgetting the kinetic-energy term in the nozzle energy equation, e.g. setting $T_3=T_2$.

## How it is graded

Part a: +2 general steady-flow equation, +2 fan reduced equation, +2 ideal-gas constant-$c_p$ relation, +1 final $T_2$. Part b: +2 mass conservation setup, +2 $V_2=V_1$, +1 outlet density, +2 nozzle energy equation, +2 ideal-gas relation, +1 final $T_3$. Assumptions are also awarded partial credit.
