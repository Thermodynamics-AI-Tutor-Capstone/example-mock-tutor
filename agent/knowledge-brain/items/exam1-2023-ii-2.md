# Exam 1 (Summer 2023), problem II.2: Isobaric heating of saturated liquid water

## Problem

Water begins at State 1 in a saturated liquid state at 2 MPa and is heated in an isobaric process until a temperature of 600 K at State 2.

a) Write the assumptions necessary to solve for State 2 and the heat and work.
b) What is the temperature of the water at State 1 in Kelvin?
c) What is the phase of the water at State 2?
d) What is the work done during this process in kilojoules if the mass inside the cylinder is 1 kg?
e) What is the heat added to the system from State 1 to State 2 in kilojoules if the mass inside the cylinder is 1 kg?

## What it tests

- Students can use saturation tables to find $T_{sat}$ from pressure.
- Students can determine phase by comparing temperature to saturation temperature.
- Students can calculate constant-pressure boundary work using specific volumes.
- Students can apply the first law to find heat transfer from internal energy and work.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- S.C.S.
- quasi-equilibrium process
- $\Delta KE = \Delta PE = 0$

## Where students go wrong

- Looking up saturation temperature at 600 K instead of at the given 2 MPa for State 1.
- Using compressed-liquid tables instead of saturated-liquid tables for State 1.
- Treating State 2 as a saturated mixture instead of superheated vapor because $T_2 > T_{sat}$ at 2 MPa.
- Using $\Delta h$ instead of $\Delta u$ when applying the first law.
- Forgetting the isobaric work term $P(v_2-v_1)$ or assigning the wrong sign.
- Using $c_p$ directly to compute heat instead of the first law with $\Delta u$ and work.

## How it is graded

Part a +2 for assumptions; part b +2 for $T_1$; part c +2 for phase; part d awards +2 for the integral expression, +2 for the isobaric product $MP(v_2-v_1)$, +1 for each table value, and +2 for the work final. Part e awards +2 for the first-law relation, +1 for each internal-energy table value, and +2 for the heat final.
