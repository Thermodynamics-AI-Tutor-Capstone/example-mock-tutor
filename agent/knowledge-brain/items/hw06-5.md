## Problem

The gas turbine in University Park's West Campus Steam plant runs constantly to provide campus with power and steam. We've collected data on the engine's compressor for a few days in June – see the data sheet attached in the Homework 6 assignment in Canvas. Use this data sheet to calculate the work done by the compressor to compress air in the engine. Assume a mass flow rate of 30 kg/s and the following values for air: $R = 287$ J/kg-K and $c_v = 714$ J/kg-K. a. List all your assumptions on your paper as well as the method you're going to use to calculate these values in the spreadsheet. b. Plot the work done by the compressor as a function of time. BE CAREFUL ABOUT UNITS – the units on the spreadsheet are not the ones you should use in the calculation. c. Discuss this trend – what's changing with time and why do you think that is? Do some research on how a gas turbine works – what role does the compressor play in the machine? List your sources for where you found info on gas turbines.

## What it tests

- Students can model a compressor as a steady-flow, adiabatic device with negligible kinetic and potential energy changes.
- Students can compute compressor work from inlet and outlet temperatures using $\dot{W} = -\dot{m}c_p(T_2 - T_1)$.
- Students can convert spreadsheet temperature and pressure data to SI units before calculating work.
- Students can plot compressor work versus time and discuss the physical role of the compressor in a gas turbine.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- air is an ideal gas
- $c_p$ and $c_v$ are constant
- quasi-equilibrium
- steady flow
- $\Delta ke = \Delta pe = 0$
- $\dot{Q} = 0$

## Where students go wrong

- Forgetting to convert the spreadsheet temperature, pressure, or other quantities to SI units before using them.
- Using $c_v$ instead of $c_p$ in the compressor work expression.
- Getting the sign of compressor work wrong; the course sign convention gives $\dot{W} = -\dot{m}c_p(T_2 - T_1)$, so compression work is negative.
- Including heat transfer or kinetic/potential energy terms when the stated assumptions set them to zero.
- Using $c_p$ directly from the supplied values when only $R$ and $c_v$ are given; $c_p = c_v + R$ must be used.
- Plotting the raw spreadsheet values without unit conversion, or not labeling the plot.

## How it is graded

Credit is given for assumptions, the steady-flow energy balance, the compressor relation, recognizing $c_p = c_v + R$, unit conversions in the plot, and a reasonable trend discussion with sources. Numerical results depend on the attached data sheet, which is not included in the transcript.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $\dot{W}_{compressor}$: -\dot{m} c_p (T_2 - T_1) W
- $c_p$: c_v + R = 1001 J/kg-K
