# Exam 2 (Summer 2021), problem I.2: Sign of entropy change for three processes

## Problem

Identify the correct sign of the change in entropy for the following three processes. (a) Fluid undergoes a reversible cooling process: greater than zero, less than zero, or equal to zero? (b) An ideal gas undergoes an irreversible heating process: greater than zero, less than zero, or equal to zero? (c) An ideal gas undergoes an adiabatic and reversible expansion: greater than zero, less than zero, or equal to zero? (d) Use an equation to explain the answers to the previous three questions.

## What it tests

- Students can determine the sign of entropy change for reversible/irreversible cooling, heating, and adiabatic reversible processes using the entropy balance.

## Assumptions the solution expects



## Where students go wrong

- Thinking a reversible process always has $\Delta S = 0$; reversible cooling has heat transfer out, so $\delta Q < 0$.
- Thinking an irreversible heating process could have $\Delta S = 0$ because heat is added; irreversibility adds positive entropy generation.
- Forgetting that adiabatic and reversible together make the entropy-generation term and heat-transfer term zero.
- Writing the entropy balance without the entropy-generation term $\Phi$.

## How it is graded

Each multiple-choice sign is 2 pts; the equation form earns 3 pts and identifying the irreversibility/entropy-generation term as positive earns 3 pts.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $Part a: reversible cooling sign of $\Delta S$: LESS THAN ZERO
- $Part b: irreversible heating sign of $\Delta S$: GREATER THAN ZERO
- $Part c: adiabatic reversible expansion sign of $\Delta S$: EQUAL TO ZERO
- Part d: entropy balance equation: $\Delta S = \int \frac{\delta Q}{T} + \Phi$, with $\Phi > 0$
