# Homework 2 (Summer 2023), problem 4: Specific heat of diatomic oxygen vs monatomic neon

## Problem

Explain why a diatomic molecule like oxygen – $\text{O}_2$ – has a higher specific heat than a noble gas like neon – Ne – in units of $\text{J}/\text{kmol}\cdot\text{K}$.

## What it tests

- Students can explain how molecular degrees of freedom affect specific heat.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Oxygen is a diatomic molecule with vibration, rotation, translation, and electronic modes of energy storage.
- Neon is a monatomic noble gas with translation and electronic modes of energy storage.

## Where students go wrong

- Attributing the difference to molar mass alone.
- Not listing the modes available to each species.
- Missing vibration or rotation modes for diatomic oxygen.
- Forgetting that specific heat is on a molar basis, so number of atoms per molecule matters.

## How it is graded

Points: oxygen modes (+2), neon modes (+2), explanation linking more energy storage modes to higher heat capacity (+6).

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Explanation: An oxygen molecule has more ways to store energy (vibration, rotation, translation, electronic) than a neon atom (translation, electronic), so it has a higher heat capacity.
