# Exam 2 (Summer 2023), problem II.1: Entropy change of helium during a process

## Problem

Helium slowly undergoes a process from \(T_1 = 800\) K and \(P_1 = 0.1\) MPa to \(T_2 = 850\) K and \(P_2 = 1\) MPa. Answer the following questions about this process using the following properties for helium: \(R = 2077.3\) J/kg-K and \(c_p = 5190\) J/kg-K.

a) (3 points) What assumptions can you make about this fluid and process to solve for the properties of the fluid at both states?

b) (6 points) What is the change in mass-specific entropy of the fluid during this process in [J/kg-K]?

c) (6 points) Can this process be an adiabatic process? Explain why or why not – use an equation to support your answer.

## What it tests

- Students can apply the constant-specific-heat ideal-gas entropy-change equation.
- Students can compute entropy change from two independent states.
- Students can use the entropy balance to decide whether a process can be adiabatic.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Ideal gas.
- Constant specific heat \(c_p\).
- Quasi-equilibrium process, implied by the gas slowly undergoing the process.

## Where students go wrong

- Using the wrong ideal-gas entropy relation, such as one with \(c_v\) or with \(V_2/V_1\) instead of the pressure-ratio form.
- Taking the pressure ratio as \(P_1/P_2\) instead of \(P_2/P_1\), producing a sign error.
- Arithmetic error in evaluating \(\ln(T_2/T_1)\) or \(\ln(P_2/P_1)\).
- Saying the process can be adiabatic because entropy generation can be negative; \(\Phi\) cannot be negative.
- Assuming adiabatic implies isentropic, so \(\Delta s\) would be zero, and then not using the full entropy balance to explain why a negative \(\Delta s\) requires heat transfer.

## How it is graded

Part (a): 1 pt each for ideal gas, constant \(c_p\), and quasi-equilibrium. Part (b): +4 for correct entropy-change equation, +2 for numerical answer. Part (c): +2 for entropy balance, +1 for sign of heat-transfer term, +1 for nonnegative entropy generation, +2 for concluding that \(\Delta s<0\) requires heat transfer.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Change in mass-specific entropy: -1468.5 J/kg-K
- Can this process be adiabatic: No
