## Problem

Water in a piston-cylinder system undergoes an isobaric heat addition process. It starts as a saturated liquid at 438.1 K (State 1), and then heat is added. Indicate the table used for each question. h. What is the enthalpy at State 1? i. What is the enthalpy when the water reaches a saturated vapor state? j. What is the enthalpy when the water reaches 700 K? What is the phase?

## What it tests

- Students can use quality $x=0$ and $x=1$ to enter the saturation table.
- Students can relate saturation temperature to pressure for an isobaric process.
- Students can distinguish saturated vapor from superheated vapor using $T > T_{sat}$.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- simple compressible system (solution writes S.L.S.)
- quasi-equilibrium process
- isobaric process

## Where students go wrong

- not using $x=0$ to get $h_f$ for the saturated liquid state.
- not recognizing that $T_1 = 438.1$ K fixes the saturation pressure at 0.7 MPa for the isobaric process.
- using the compressed-liquid table for State 1 instead of the saturation table.
- using the saturation table at 700 K instead of the superheated table.
- identifying State 3 as saturated vapor rather than superheated vapor.

## How it is graded

Points for assumptions, saturated liquid/vapor enthalpies, finding P = 0.7 MPa, and superheated vapor enthalpy/phase.

- h1: 697 kJ/kg
- h1 table: Table D.2

- h3: 3326 kJ/kg
- h3 table: Table D.3.H
