## Problem

3. (20 points) Below is a table of molecular weights of the most common substances we'll be using in this class. Use these molecular weights to answer the following questions.
| Species | Molecular weight |
| Nitrogen (N$_2$) | 28.0134 g/mol |
| Oxygen (O$_2$) | 31.9988 g/mol |
| Carbon dioxide (CO$_2$) | 44.01 g/mol |
| Water (H$_2$O) | 18.01528 g/mol |
a. What is the particular gas constant for O$_2$?
b. The mass-specific volume of a system of CO$_2$ is 10 m$^3$/kg – what is its molar-specific volume?
c. Which substance has a particular gas constant of 188.92 J/kg-K?
d. A gas cylinder has 20 kg of H$_2$O in it – how many moles of H$_2$O are in the cylinder?
e. The container has molar-specific volume of N$_2$ of 0.1 m$^3$/mol – what is the density of this nitrogen?

## What it tests

- Compute particular gas constant from molecular weight
- Convert between mass-specific volume and molar-specific volume via molecular weight
- Use particular gas constant to identify a substance
- Calculate moles from mass and molecular weight
- Convert molar-specific volume to density

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Molecular weights are as tabulated
- Universal gas constant $\bar{R}=8314.5$ J/(kmol-K)
- Relations $R=\bar{R}/m$, $\bar{V}=v m$, $\rho=m/\bar{V}$

## Where students go wrong

- Using the molecular weight of atomic oxygen or nitrogen instead of O$_2$/N$_2$
- Computing $R=\bar{R}/m$ with molecular weight in g/mol without converting to kg/kmol
- Forgetting to multiply mass-specific volume by molecular weight to get molar-specific volume
- Guessing the substance for $R=188.92$ instead of calculating $m=\bar{R}/R$
- For density, not taking the reciprocal of the specific volume or mixing g, kg, mol, and kmol
