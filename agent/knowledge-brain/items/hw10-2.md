# Homework 10 (Summer 2023), problem 2: Vapor-compression refrigeration cycle with R-134a

## Problem

A vapor compression cycle uses R-134a as a refrigerant with a mass flow of $0.1$ kg/s and runs between pressures of $0.16$ MPa and $1.8$ MPa. State 1 is a saturated vapor, and State 3 is a saturated liquid. The compressor has an isentropic efficiency of 90%. Use the NIST webbook to find the state points for R-134a.

a) What is the power required to run this cycle?

b) How much heat is extracted from the cold space?

c) What is the coefficient of performance of the cycle? How does this compare to the Carnot COP?

d) In an attempt to extract some work out of the cycle, you replace the throttle with a little turbine. Assuming the turbine is ideal and the pressure ratio across the turbine is the same as that of the throttle, how much work could you extract from the fluid? How does this change the heat extracted from the cold space assuming that State 1 is the same?

## What it tests

- Students can evaluate a vapor-compression refrigeration cycle using NIST property data.
- Students can apply steady-flow energy balances to compressor, evaporator, condenser, and throttle/turbine.
- Students can compute real compressor exit state from isentropic efficiency.
- Students can compare actual COP with Carnot COP.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- steady-flow, quasi-equilibrium control volumes
- negligible-kinetic-energy and negligible-potential-energy for all devices
- compressor: adiabatic, no external heat transfer
- condenser/evaporator: no work
- throttle: adiabatic, no work
- turbine replacement in part d: ideal turbine, so $s_4=s_3$

## Where students go wrong

- Taking compressor power as positive rather than negative: $\dot{W}_{comp}=-\dot{m}(h_2-h_1)$.
- Using $h_{2s}$ instead of the actual $h_2$ for compressor power, ignoring the 90% isentropic efficiency.
- Using $h_3$ at the evaporator inlet without recognizing $h_4=h_3$ through the throttle.
- Selecting NIST properties in the wrong phase; for example, taking saturated liquid or subcooled values at state 2 when $s_{2s}>s_g$.
- Computing Carnot COP with pressure ratios instead of saturation temperatures at the evaporator and condenser pressures.
- For the turbine replacement, using $h_4=h_3$ instead of $s_4=s_3$ and computing quality at the low pressure.

## How it is graded

Solution points reward assumptions, NIST state properties, steady-flow energy balances, compressor isentropic efficiency, and final answers. Turbine extraction part rewards state 4s quality, enthalpy, and turbine work.
