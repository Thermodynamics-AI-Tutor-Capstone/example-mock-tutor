# Homework 6 (Summer 2023), problem 2: Variable-area nozzle air acceleration

## Problem

A variable-area nozzle is used to accelerate steady-flowing air ($c_p = 1001$ J/kg-K) to different flow velocities. The air always enters the nozzle at a velocity of 10 m/s, a temperature of 350 K, and density of 1.225 kg/m$^3$, where the nozzle has an initial area of 0.02 m$^2$. a. What is the mass flow of air through the nozzle? b. Plot the temperature of the air leaving the nozzle as a function of nozzle exit velocity from 20-200 m/s. Show your calculation steps on your homework paper and then use Excel or Matlab to do the calculations at all the points requested.

## What it tests

- Students can calculate mass flow from density, velocity, and area.
- Students can apply the steady-flow energy equation to an adiabatic, no-work nozzle.
- Students can compute outlet temperature as a function of exit velocity and plot the trend.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- air is an ideal gas
- $c_p$ is constant
- quasi-equilibrium
- steady flow
- $\Delta pe = 0$
- $\dot{Q} = 0$
- $\dot{W} = 0$

## Where students go wrong

- Computing volume flow $A_1 v_1$ and forgetting to multiply by density to get mass flow.
- Writing the outlet-temperature relation with the wrong sign; for an accelerating adiabatic nozzle the temperature drops as velocity increases.
- Using $c_v$ instead of $c_p$ for an enthalpy change.
- Keeping heat, work, or potential-energy terms in the nozzle energy balance when the stated/expected assumptions set them to zero.
- Plotting a constant or rising temperature trend instead of a decreasing curve, or not labeling units/axes.

## How it is graded

Credit is given for stating assumptions, writing the steady-flow energy balance, dropping $\Delta pe$, $\dot{Q}$, and $\dot{W}$, using the ideal-gas constant-$c_p$ enthalpy relation, computing mass flow, and making a readable plot with the correct trend.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- $\dot{m}$: 0.245 kg/s
- $T_2(v_2)$: T_1 - \frac{1}{2c_p}(v_2^2 - v_1^2) K
