## Problem

2 kg of water undergoes the cycle below. Fill in the missing state points and calculate the change in internal energy, $\Delta U$, in kJ for each step. Then plot the cycle on a T-v diagram, including the vapor dome.

| State | P [MPa] | T [K] | v [m$^3$/kg] | $\Delta U$ [kJ] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 |  | 0.0012864 | 1-2: |
| 2 | 10 | 560 |  | 2-3: |
| 3 | 10 |  | 0.020091 | 3-4: |
| 4 | 5 |  | 0.039446 | 4-1: |

## What it tests

- Students can identify saturated liquid and saturated vapor states from specific volume.
- Students can use compressed-liquid and superheated vapor tables to find missing properties.
- Students can compute total internal energy change from specific internal energies and mass.
- Students can sketch a cycle on a T-v diagram with the vapor dome.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- simple compressible system
- quasi-equilibrium processes

## Where students go wrong

- using table internal energies $u$ directly as $\Delta U$ without multiplying by 2 kg.
- failing to use $v_f$ and $v_g$ at the given pressure to identify saturated liquid and saturated vapor states.
- using the printed $T_2 = 560$ K table lookup instead of the solution's $T_2 = 500$ K, or vice versa; the solution uses 500 K.
- computing $\Delta U$ with sign errors or in kJ/kg rather than kJ.
- omitting the vapor dome or drawing paths inconsistent with the saturated liquid/vapor dome.

## How it is graded

Points for S.C.S. and quasi-equilibrium assumptions, table lookups, mass-weighted internal-energy changes, and the T-v diagram. Note: solution uses T2 = 500 K although the printed table lists 560 K.
