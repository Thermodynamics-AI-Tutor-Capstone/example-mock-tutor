## Problem

Helium is placed in a piston-cylinder system and is isothermally heated from an initial volume $\mathcal{V}_1$ to a final volume $\mathcal{V}_2$.

a) Choose the correct statement about the change in internal energy of the helium.
A. $U_1=U_2$
B. $U_1>U_2$
C. $U_1<U_2$

b) Choose the correct statement about $\mathcal{V}_1$ and $\mathcal{V}_2$.
A. $\mathcal{V}_1=\mathcal{V}_2$
B. $\mathcal{V}_1>\mathcal{V}_2$
C. $\mathcal{V}_1<\mathcal{V}_2$

c) What is the sign of work from State 1 to State 2?
A. Positive
B. Negative

d) Explain your answer to the previous question. Use an equation to explain your answer.

## What it tests

- Students can use ideal-gas internal energy to analyze an isothermal process.
- Students can infer volume change from the sign of heat or work.
- Students can use the first law to relate heat and work for an isothermal ideal-gas process.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Helium is an ideal gas.
- For an ideal gas, $du=c_v\,dT$.
- Heating means ${}_1Q_2>0$.
- First law: $\Delta U={}_1Q_2-{}_1W_2$.

## Where students go wrong

- Assuming heating increases internal energy for an ideal gas.
- Choosing negative work for a heating isothermal process.
- Treating $\Delta U$ as nonzero for an ideal gas at constant temperature.
- Not connecting the sign of heat to the sign of work through the first law.

## How it is graded

Ideal-gas assumption 1 pt; $du=c_v dT$ 1 pt; heating sign 1 pt; first-law relation 1 pt; multiple-choice answers 2 pts each.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Internal energy change: A. $U_1=U_2$
- Volume comparison: C. $\mathcal{V}_1<\mathcal{V}_2$
- Work sign: A. Positive
- Explanation: ${}_1Q_2={}_1W_2$ and both are positive
