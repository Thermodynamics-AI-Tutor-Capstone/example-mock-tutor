## Problem

An engineer develops a new surface coating for the blades of a water pump that reduces friction and turbulent mixing in the pump. For a given pressure ratio of the pump, answer the following questions. (a) Does the isentropic efficiency of the pump increase or decrease with the new coating? (b) Does the work required to drive the pump increase or decrease with the new coating? (c) Explain your answer to the previous two questions. Use an equation to support your discussion.

## What it tests

- Students can relate reduced irreversibilities to isentropic efficiency and real compressor/pump work input.

## Assumptions the solution expects



## Where students go wrong

- Using the turbine efficiency definition instead of the pump/compressor efficiency definition.
- Writing $\eta_{pump} = \dot{W}_{real}/\dot{W}_{ideal}$ instead of $\eta_{pump} = \dot{W}_{ideal}/\dot{W}_{real}$.
- Saying efficiency decreases because friction is reduced.
- Not connecting reduced friction/mixing to reduced irreversibilities and lower real work input.

## How it is graded

Two multiple-choice answers worth 2 pts each; explanation with ideal-vs-real work equation earns 3 pts and explanation that reduced irreversibilities raise efficiency and lower real work earns 3 pts.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Isentropic efficiency: INCREASE
- Required work: DECREASE
