# Homework 7 (Summer 2023), problem 4: Second law statements for a power cycle

## Problem

While the First Law tells us that energy must be conserved, the Second Law tells us the limits of energy exchange. Write each statement of the Second Law in your own words. In doing so, explain what it means for a power cycle (heat engine) and what limits it places on the operation or performance of that machine. If you need an example, use a car engine; that engine runs the Otto cycle. (p. 2)

## What it tests

- Students can paraphrase the Second Law in multiple equivalent statements.
- Students can explain how each statement limits a power cycle.
- Students can connect irreversibility to entropy generation in a cycle.

## Assumptions the solution expects



## Where students go wrong

- Saying the Second Law only says heat cannot be converted to work without stating that some heat must be rejected.
- Asserting a perfect heat engine with $\eta_{th}=1$ is possible if friction is eliminated.
- Writing the statements without relating them to a power cycle or heat engine.
- Confusing irreversibility with friction only, rather than entropy generation.
- Omitting the entropy-generation or equilibrium statement.

## How it is graded

Each of four statements +2; implication +1 per statement; Statement 1 has an additional +1 for correctly noting $\eta_{th}<1$.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Statement 1: You cannot devise a power cycle where heat is completely converted to work; some heat must be rejected. Implication: $\eta_{th}<1$; even without friction, not all fuel energy can be converted to work.
- Statement 2: Processes have a natural directionality; moving a system opposite its spontaneous direction requires work input. Implication: a cycle will always include some non-spontaneous processes, so work input is always needed.
- Statement 3: If losses/irreversibilities are present, entropy will be generated. Implication: in a cycle this increases the entropy of the surroundings.
- Statement 4: Spontaneous processes move systems toward equilibrium where entropy is maximized. Implication: losses and irreversibilities are inescapable.
