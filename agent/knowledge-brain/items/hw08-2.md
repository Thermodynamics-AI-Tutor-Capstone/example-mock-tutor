# Homework 8 (Summer 2023), problem 2: Reversible Carnot cycle entropy and work

## Problem

A reversible Carnot cycle operates between a high-temperature reservoir of $T_H = 800 \ \mathrm{K}$ and a low-temperature reservoir of $T_L = 300 \ \mathrm{K}$. If the heat rejected from the cycle is equal to $10 \ \mathrm{kJ}$, calculate the following: (a) the thermal efficiency of the cycle; (b) the change in entropy of the system during heat addition; (c) the change in entropy of the system during heat rejection; (d) the net work of the cycle.

## What it tests

- Students can compute Carnot thermal efficiency from reservoir temperatures.
- Students can calculate entropy change for reversible isothermal heat transfer.
- Students can relate heat rejected to heat added for a reversible Carnot cycle.
- Students can compute net work from thermal efficiency and heat added.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- reversible
- steady-state
- quasi-equilibrium processes

## Where students go wrong

- Not assuming reversible, thus not equating thermal efficiency to Carnot efficiency.
- Treating heat rejected as positive, which would give positive entropy change during rejection.
- Using $Q_L/Q_H = T_H/T_L$ inverted, e.g. getting $Q_H = Q_L T_L/T_H$.
- Converting kJ to J incorrectly when computing entropy change.
- Using $W_{net} = Q_H - Q_L$ with signs/magnitudes instead of $W_{net} = \eta_{th} Q_H$.

## How it is graded

Points for stating reversible/steady/quasi-equilibrium, using Carnot efficiency, relating Q_H and Q_L, using ΔS=Q/T, and net work.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- thermal efficiency: 0.625
- entropy change during heat addition: 33.333 J/K
- entropy change during heat rejection: -33.333 J/K
- net work: 16666.875 J
