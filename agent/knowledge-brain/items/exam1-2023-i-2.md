# Exam 1 (Summer 2023), problem I.2: Interpreting quality for saturated water

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

## Problem

Two systems of water, A and B, are at the same temperature but have different qualities: $x_A=0.2$ and $x_B=0.8$.

a. Which system has a greater mass of vapor?
b. Explain your answer to part a.
c. Which system has the greater internal energy?
d. Explain your answer to part c.

## What it tests

- Students can interpret quality as a vapor mass fraction.
- Students can compare internal energy of saturated mixtures using the mixture rule.
- Students can reason about saturated mixture properties at the same temperature.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- both systems are at the same temperature
- both systems are saturated mixtures

## Where students go wrong

- Assuming quality equals total vapor mass rather than vapor mass fraction.
- Using $u = x u_g$ only and omitting the liquid contribution.
- Ignoring that at the same temperature, both systems have the same $u_f$ and $u_g$.
- Comparing total internal energy without recognizing the mixture-rule dependence on quality.

## How it is graded

Parts a and c are each +2; parts b and d are each +3. Part b is awarded for the definition of quality; part d is awarded for the mixture internal-energy expression.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- Part a: system with greater mass of vapor: System B
- Part b: explanation: $x = \dfrac{M_{vap}}{M_{liq}+M_{vap}}$, so larger quality means larger vapor mass fraction.
- Part c: system with greater internal energy: System B
- Part d: explanation: $u = x u_g + (1-x)u_f$, and $u_g > u_f$; therefore $u_B > u_A$.
