## Problem

Consider these five conservation scenarios and provide: indicate whether it is appropriate to use a finite-time or instantaneous conservation formulation; write the form of the conservation equation, cancelling any terms not required and explaining why they can be neglected. a. One way to conserve water is to catch it in a rain barrel to water your plants and lawn. In this scenario, a rain bucket with a volume $V$ collects rain at a rate of $\dot{m}_{rain}$ during a rain storm. Assume the bucket doesn't overflow. Write a conservation of mass statement for the water in this scenario.

## What it tests

- Students can choose finite-time vs instantaneous conservation formulations based on flow rates.
- Students can simplify a mass conservation equation by canceling absent terms.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- Instantaneous formulation because the statement includes a flow rate.
- No outflow because the bucket does not overflow.
- No mass generation.

## Where students go wrong

- Using finite-time instead of instantaneous for a flow-rate statement.
- Leaving a mass outflow term even though the bucket does not overflow.
- Including a mass generation term.

## How it is graded

+2 for instantaneous; +2 for generic conservation equation; +2 for final simplified equation.

## Final answers (hidden — for checking only)

- formulation: instantaneous
- conservation of mass equation: \frac{dH_{water}}{dt} = \dot{m}_{in}
