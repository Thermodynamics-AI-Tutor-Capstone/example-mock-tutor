## Problem

In this problem, you will compare the Otto and Diesel cycles for a few different conditions to help understand the differences between the two cycles and how they're typically used in piston engines.

| Air standard, ideal Otto cycle: | Air standard, ideal Diesel cycle: |
|---|---|
| • Adiabatic, reversible compression | • Adiabatic, reversible compression |
| • Isochoric heat addition | • Isobaric heat addition |
| • Adiabatic, reversible expansion | • Adiabatic, reversible expansion |
| • Isochoric heat rejection | • Isochoric heat rejection |

In all cases, you will assume the intake conditions (state 1) to be the following:
- $T_1 = 300$ K
- $P_1 = 0.1$ MPa
- All cases have a bottom dead-center volume ($\mathcal{V}_1$) of 0.625 L.
- Assume air to have the following properties: $\gamma=1.4$, $c_v=714$ J/kgK, $c_p=1000$ J/kgK

Additionally, you will use a heat addition of 1500 J for all analyses.

a. Analyze Otto and Diesel cycles for a compression ratio of 11 (high compression ratio gasoline engine, low compression ratio diesel). Fill out the following table for these two cycles:

Otto Cycle table: State, P [MPa], T [K], $\mathcal{V}$ [m³] for states 1, 2, 3, 4.

Diesel Cycle table: State, P [MPa], T [K], $\mathcal{V}$ [m³] for states 1, 2, 3, 4.

b. Draw the P-v diagram for both cycles on the same plot (to scale as best you can). Show the net work on both and indicate which cycle has higher net work.

c. What is the efficiency of each of these cycles?

## What it tests

- Students can evaluate ideal air-standard Otto and Diesel cycles with constant specific heats.
- Students can compute state properties from ideal-gas relations, compression ratio, and heat addition.
- Students can draw and compare P-v diagrams and identify net work areas.
- Students can compute net work and thermal efficiency for both cycles.

## Assumptions the solution expects

- air-standard cycle
- quasi-equilibrium
- reversible
- ideal gas
- constant specific heats
- constant mass
- negligible-kinetic-energy and negligible-potential-energy

## Where students go wrong

- Using liters without converting to $\mathcal{V}_1=0.000625\ \mathrm{m^3}$ when computing mass.
- Computing mass from $P_1\mathcal{V}_1/(R T_1)$ with pressure in MPa or volume in L without unit conversion.
- Using $c_v$ instead of $c_p$ for Diesel heat addition or missing the constant-pressure boundary work.
- Using $c_p$ instead of $c_v$ for Otto isochoric heat addition.
- Reversing the compression ratio, for example $\mathcal{V}_2 = r\mathcal{V}_1$ instead of $\mathcal{V}_1/r$.
- For the Diesel state 3 volume, forgetting $P_3=P_2$ and using the ideal-gas law incorrectly.
- Drawing both cycles with the same heat-addition path rather than showing Otto as isochoric and Diesel as isobaric.
- Computing Otto net work as if heat addition performs boundary work, despite the isochoric process.
- The solution transcript has an Otto $P_2$ box as $2.87\times10^4$ Pa even though the preceding formula gives MPa; the Diesel section uses $P_2=2.87$ MPa.

## How it is graded

Solution points reward assumptions, mass calculation, state properties, first-law balances for heat addition and work, P-v diagrams with net work, and final efficiencies/net works. The Otto $P_2$ box has a clear unit slip in the transcript.
