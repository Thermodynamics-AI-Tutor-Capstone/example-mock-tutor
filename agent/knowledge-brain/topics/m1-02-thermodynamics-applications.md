# M1.2 — Thermodynamics Applications

## What this lecture covers

This lecture introduces the scope of engineering thermodynamics through everyday and engineered examples, then distinguishes classical thermodynamics as an empirical science from statistical thermodynamics.

## Key ideas

- Thermodynamics is everywhere: the slide collage includes wind turbines, waterfalls, iced drinks, a car, a circuit board, valves/pipes, a cyclist, a nebula, an atomic-model sculpture, sand dunes, an airplane wing and solar panels (p. 2).
- Phase change is a thermodynamic application: the kettle shows liquid to vapor/gas, and the annotations add solid to liquid and solid to vapor (p. 3).
- Compression and expansion involve energy transfer: a bicycle pump/tire is labeled “energy in by doing work,” and a turbine/compressor interior is labeled “energy out” (p. 4).
- Cycles return the working fluid to its original state; the examples are an internal combustion engine and outdoor air-conditioning/heat-pump condenser units (p. 5).
- Friction loss converts work into heat: the examples are matches and a disc brake (p. 6).
- Classical thermodynamics is largely empirical: observation and experiment lead to laws, and engineering applies the laws; statistical thermodynamics is shown as a separate approach connected by Boltzmann (p. 7).

## Examples in this lecture

- Boiling water in an electric kettle demonstrates liquid-to-vapor phase change (p. 3).
- A floor pump inflating a bicycle tire and a turbine/compressor interior demonstrate compression/expansion with work-energy transfer (p. 4).
- An internal combustion engine and outdoor condenser units demonstrate cycles in which the fluid returns to its original state (p. 5).
- Matches and a car disc brake demonstrate friction loss producing heat (p. 6).
