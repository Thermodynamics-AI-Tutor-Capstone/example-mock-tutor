## What this lecture covers

This opening lecture sets up the entire ME 300 course. It explains the course goal, the module sequence, the course-level competencies, the active-learning/metacognition model, the assessment structure, and where to find course resources.

## Key ideas

- The stated course goal is to introduce concepts of energy and conservation principles and apply them to real-world systems (p. 2).
- The course is organized as eight modules: Module 1, Introductory Principles; Module 2, Properties, States, and Processes; Module 3, Properties of Gases; Module 4, Phase Changes; Module 5, Energy Conservation and the First Law; Module 6, Steady-Flow Energy Equation; Module 7, The Second Law; and Module 8, Cycle Analysis (p. 3).
- The slide lists course-level learning objectives: obtain thermodynamic data and use appropriate approximations, write First Law forms for closed systems and control volumes, solve First Law problems with a single answer, make assumptions for real-world First Law problems, write Second Law forms for closed systems and control volumes, apply the Second Law to performance limitations, and relate component performance to overall system performance (p. 4).
- The course uses an active learning and metacognition cycle: the student absorbs content, does something active, reflects on learning and performance, then improves (p. 5).
- Assessments are homework at 25%, explained examples at 15%, quizzes at 20%, and exams at 40%; the slide gives details about each category (p. 6).
- Resources are Canvas, the Turns and Pauley textbook and its additional Cambridge videos, and the ideal gas game (p. 7).
