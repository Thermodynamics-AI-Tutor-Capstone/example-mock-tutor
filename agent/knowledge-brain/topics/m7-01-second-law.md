# M7.1 — Second Law

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

## What this lecture covers
This lecture introduces the Second Law by revisiting the First Law energy balance and then emphasizing what it cannot do: prescribe process direction. It also gives historical context from early steam-engine development through Carnot’s 1824 paper.

## Key ideas
- The finite energy balance is $\Delta E = {}_1Q_2 - {}_1W_2$; the rate form is $\frac{dE}{dt} = \dot{Q} - \dot{W}$ (p. 2).
- Energy is conserved, and it is exchanged through heat transfer and work transfer (p. 2).
- The lecture explicitly marks “NO” to the question of whether the First Law prescribes the direction of a process (p. 3).
- Newcomen (1712) and Watt (1765) developed and improved steam-engine technology before theory could explain it (p. 4).
- Sadi Carnot (1824), frustrated with steam-engine technology, wrote a paper on the fundamental principles of the Second Law (p. 5).

## Notation used
- $E$: total energy.
- $Q$ and $W$: energy exchanged as heat and work; in this course’s process notation these are written ${}_1Q_2$ and ${}_1W_2$.
- $\dot{Q}$ and $\dot{W}$: rates of heat and work transfer.
- $\Delta E$ and $dE/dt$: change of energy and rate of change of energy.

## What students get wrong here
- The lecture explicitly pushes back on the idea that conservation of energy tells you which way a process can proceed: the First Law alone does not prescribe direction (p. 3).
