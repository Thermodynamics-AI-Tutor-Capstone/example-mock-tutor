## What the course is

Thermodynamics concepts, properties of pure substances, and first- and second-law analysis of
systems and control volumes. Prerequisites CHEM 110 and MATH 141. The Summer 2023 section was a
12-week, fully online, asynchronous course: short lecture videos with annotated slides (the
decks in `lectures/`), weekly homework, frequent Canvas quizzes, paired "explained examples", and
three exams.

## Textbook — this matters more than it looks

**Turns & Pauley, *Thermodynamics — Concepts and Applications*, 2nd edition, Cambridge
University Press, 2020.** Key `turns-pauley-2e`. Property tables are cited as Table D.1, D.2,
D.3 … (steam) in the course's solutions.

Most thermodynamics material on the open internet assumes **Cengel & Boles**, whose chapter
numbering is different. Never cite a bare "Chapter 3": name the lecture (M-number) or the section
from the card you read.

## Sign convention and notation

Heat **into** the system and work **out of** the system are positive:
$\Delta E_{sys} = {}_1Q_2 - {}_1W_2$ (M5.4). Process quantities carry pre-subscripts
(${}_1Q_2$, ${}_1W_2$). Entropy generation is $\Phi$: $\Delta S = \int \delta Q/T + \Phi$ (M7.7,
Exam 2 solutions). Coefficient of performance is $\beta$. Total volume is $\mathcal{V}$.

## How the instructor's solutions are laid out

The syllabus does not prescribe a format, but every posted solution follows the same shape, and
awards points for each part of it: **Given → Find → Assume** (each assumption stated, one point
each) **→ state by state** (fix each state from two independent properties, look up the rest)
**→ conservation equation** with cancelled terms **→ boxed answer with units.** Coach students
into that shape; a right number by an incoherent route loses most of the credit.
