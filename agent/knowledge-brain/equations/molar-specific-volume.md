# Molar-specific volume

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

$$
\bar{v} = \frac{\mathcal{V}}{N}
$$

Molar-specific volume is total volume per mole, $\bar{v} = \mathcal{V}/N$. It is the molar analog of mass-specific volume. Use it when working on a molar basis, such as when using molar internal energy, molar enthalpy, or the molar gas constant. The bar over a symbol in the course indicates a molar property. Here $N$ is the number of moles, not the number of molecules. This relation lets you convert between total volume and amount of substance on a mole basis; the corresponding mass-specific relation uses mass $M$ instead.
