# State postulate for simple compressible substances

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

$$
\Phi_1 = f(\Phi_2, \Phi_3)
$$

The equation states that for a simple compressible substance, one intensive property can be determined by two other independent intensive properties. It explains why property tables list quantities such as u, h, or s as functions of T and P or T and v. The two independent properties cannot be redundant. The lecture notes specifically point out that density and specific volume are not independent because rho = 1/v. This is not an equation for calculation, but the principle behind using two properties to enter property tables.

**Also written in the lectures as:**

- $\mathcal{P}_1 = f(\mathcal{P}_2, \mathcal{P}_3)$
