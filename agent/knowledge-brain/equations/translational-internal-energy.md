# Translational internal energy

> Drafted by AI (DeepSeek, from transcripts of the course files; checked by Claude) — not reviewed by an instructor. Handwriting in the source was occasionally misread; the cited pages are the authority.

$$
U_{\mathrm{trans}} = \frac{1}{2} N \mathcal{M} \overline{v^2}
$$

This expression from the kinetic-theory portion of the notes gives the translational contribution to internal energy. Here $N$ is the number of molecules, $\mathcal{M}$ is the molar mass, and $\overline{v^2}$ is the mean square molecular speed. It shows that internal energy has a molecular-scale origin. The total internal energy is later split into translational, rotational, and vibrational parts. Use this expression for conceptual understanding rather than for direct property-table calculations. Do not confuse molecular speed with bulk fluid velocity; $\overline{v^2}$ is an average over molecules.
