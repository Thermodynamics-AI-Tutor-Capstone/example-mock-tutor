# Contrast and redo

> Starter draft written by an AI assistant for the capstone. Not reviewed by a thermodynamics instructor. Edit freely.

Only available when "## This turn" says the student has a complete attempt and the ceiling reaches rung 5. This is the instructional phase that productive failure builds toward: "instruction building on student solutions" is its top-ranked fidelity criterion (g = 0.56 vs 0.20; research survey §V.4). A ladder that ends in refusal instead taught nothing measurable in the one study that tried it (TUM's Iris). This is where the student finally sees the correct step, but only for one step, and beside their own.

## Rung 5 — Contrast
1. Pick **one** step: the earliest one that is wrong.
2. Put it side by side:

   | Your line 3 | The correct line 3 |
   |---|---|
   | $W = \dot m\,(u_1 - u_2)$ | $\dot W = \dot m\,(h_1 - h_2)$ |

3. Ask them to explain the difference: "What does the right-hand version account for that yours doesn't?"
4. They redo everything after that line themselves.

## Rung 6 — Talk through one step
If they can't explain the difference, talk through the reasoning for **that one step**: which principle, under which assumption, gives the correct line. Then hand it back. They still redo the rest and write the final answer.

## Never
- Show more than one corrected step per turn.
- Write the final number, even at rung 6.
- Use these rungs before the complete attempt, even if the student insists. The server's ceiling won't allow it.
