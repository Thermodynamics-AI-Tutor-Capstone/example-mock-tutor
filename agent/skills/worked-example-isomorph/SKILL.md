# Worked example of a different problem

> Starter draft written by an AI assistant for the capstone. Not reviewed by a thermodynamics instructor. Edit freely.

For *choosing a model or an assumption* (closed vs control volume, which terms vanish, ideal gas vs tables), KLI's mapping is worked examples and contrasting cases, not Socratic questioning (research survey, finding 3). The example must be **isomorphic**: it teaches the same decision, but copying it can't solve their problem.

## Build it
1. **Name the move** the student is missing, in one phrase: "writing the steady-flow energy balance for a device with work out."
2. **Change the surface, keep the decision.** Pairs that work:
   - turbine ↔ compressor or pump (work out ↔ work in)
   - throttle ↔ nozzle (both adiabatic with no work; the nozzle keeps ΔKE)
   - rigid tank ↔ piston-cylinder (no boundary work ↔ boundary work)
   - steam ↔ R-134a ↔ air (tables ↔ ideal gas)
   - a different fluid, different pressures, different numbers
3. **Never reuse their numbers or their device** with only the numbers changed. If one substitution turns your example into their answer, it's too close.

## Present it
- The ME 300 format, short: KNOWN / FIND / ASSUMPTIONS (each justified by words in *your* problem) / ANALYSIS / SANITY CHECK.
- Stop and highlight the decision point: "**This is the step that matters:** the valve does no work and has no heat transfer, so h₂ = h₁."
- Approximate property values, labelled approximate. Or leave symbols, since the move is the point, not the numbers.

## Hand it back
End with their problem, not yours: "Now your turbine. Which line of that example matches your line 3?" They do the transfer.
