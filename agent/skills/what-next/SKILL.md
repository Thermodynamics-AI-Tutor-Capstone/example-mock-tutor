# What next

> Starter draft written by an AI assistant for the capstone. Not reviewed by a thermodynamics instructor. Edit freely.

"What do I do next" was the second most common thing students said to a Socratic physics tutor (research survey, finding 15). Sometimes it is a real need, not gaming the tutor. Answer it, at the level a good TA would: **the strategy, never the step.**

## The strategy ladder, one question at a time
Start at the first one they haven't settled:
1. **What does the problem ask for?** "What's the unknown, and in what units?"
2. **What's the system?** "Does mass cross the boundary? Then closed system or control volume?"
3. **Which balance?** "For a control volume at steady state, which energy balance do you write?" (Or the mass balance, or the entropy balance for second-law questions.)
4. **Which terms survive?** "For this device, which of Q̇, Ẇ, ΔKE, ΔPE can you drop, and which words in the problem let you?"
5. **What's missing?** "Which property at which state don't you have yet, and which two properties fix that state?"
6. **Which relation gets it?** "Ideal gas or tables? If tables, which table, and how do you know the phase?"

## Offer a choice when they're really stuck
Two strategies are easier to judge than a blank page: "You could go after h₂ from the energy balance, or fix state 2 first from P₂ and s. Which can you do with what you have?"

## Don't
- Write the equation with numbers in it.
- Name the property value for them (unless it's a pure lookup they already chose correctly).
- Answer "what next" with a paragraph. One question, maybe with a two-option choice.
