# Round 2: ten simulated students after the fixes (2026-09-23)

The same ten accounts (`test1`–`test10@tutor.test`) came back as returning students, with new topics
and new hidden misconceptions (`eval/students-r2.yml`). The persona file covers four cards round 1
never tested (m01, m02, m07, m11), one card in a new setting (m15), three misconceptions with no card,
and two controls with nothing to fix (Priya, Devon). There were 30 new chats and 186 student messages,
on production after PRs #7–#9 (`main` at f946223, Jev picks the style). Round 1's chats are untouched.

**Who played what:** Claude (Opus) played the students this time, not Sonnet. Two Opus reviewer agents
graded every chat as ME 300 instructors and checked steam values against the property engine. No real
students were involved, and nothing here measures learning.

Raw data (git-ignored): `data/sim-runs/r2/`. It holds the transcripts with decisions inline,
`report.md`, `review-A.md`, `review-B.md` and the debriefs.

## What the fixes did

- **Table values are right now.** Every steam value Kelvin confirmed matched the CoolProp tables to
  the printed digits, which fits it using `property_lookup` (the transcripts don't show tool calls).
- **The glitch class is gone.** No duplicated closing lines, no "Let me check…", no answering its own
  question, no emojis.
- **Misconceptions are still its strength.** It caught m11, m01, m07, m02 and m15 in their first or
  second reply every time, relapses included. It also caught all three misconceptions with no card
  (backwards quality, "a throttle always cools", the flipped compressor efficiency) even though Jev
  flagged none of them. It invented nothing for the two controls.

## What's still wrong

1. **Confident errors in its own explanations.** All 18 accuracy errors were things Kelvin said
   unprompted, not checks of the student's numbers. 6 or more were held until the student pushed
   back. Examples:
   - A higher reheat pressure "dries" the LP exit; it's the other way round.
   - s_g at 10 kPa = 7.50, "so superheated"; the table gives 8.1488 (x = 0.94). This number was
     evidently not looked up.
   - "The cycle is more sensitive to compressor efficiency"; the student's own numbers show turbine
     efficiency matters about 4× more.
   - "A high pressure ratio is chosen to maximize specific work", right after they derived that work
     peaks near a pressure ratio of 6.8.
   - Parallel flow assumed in a heat exchanger whose flow arrangement wasn't given.
   - Absolute U, measured from 0 °C, used twice to argue which body "has more energy".

   The property rule only covers table values. There's nothing yet that makes Kelvin check a claim it
   makes itself.
2. **Teach-back can reinforce a wrong belief.** With Hannah (backwards quality), Teach It Back echoed
   her wrong definition for two replies before breaking character, then told her she'd caught the
   error. She hadn't.
3. **"Mastered" and course claims come too easily.** It declared topics mastered after 2–3 answers,
   and brought up a "sample syllabus" exam schedule the student hadn't asked about.
4. **Dropped threads.** In three chats Kelvin skipped the student's own question for a turn.
5. **Style routing: 51% fit, and mostly by design.** 80 of the 92 mismatches were the router keeping
   the chat's current style. Only 12 were fresh picks. The opening style matched in 22 of 30 chats.
   The biggest miss (25 replies) was Check My Work where a Concept Check question was the right move.
   The labels come from one annotator and have not been checked.

## Next

1. **A self-check for confident claims.** Before stating a comparison or trend (a sensitivity, a
   direction, "which is larger"), Kelvin checks it with a number or hedges it. Also a sharper rule that
   any table value it states must come from `property_lookup`.
2. **Teach It Back must stop echoing a misconception.** When the student's explanation contains the
   belief itself, the misconception repair takes over within one reply.
3. **"Mastered" goes through the tool's record only**, never the model's own judgment. No unrequested
   schedule claims.
4. **Round 3 with "Kelvin picks"** on the same personas, plus loosening the rule that keeps the current
   style, to see whether style fit and helpfulness move.
5. **A teammate relabels about 40 messages** (intent, misconception, best style) to measure the
   answer key before any of these percentages go to sponsors.
